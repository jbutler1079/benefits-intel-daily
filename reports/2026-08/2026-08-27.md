### States are codifying RBP-style price caps for employer plans — Oregon's two-year track record: $107M saved, outpatient prices down 25%, but hospitals below the cap raised prices "up to it"

- **Source:** NPR national wire story, "States Try New Tactics To Bring Down High Hospital Prices," by Alex Olgin, published August 26, 2026 (story ID nx-s1-5939965). Independently corroborated across 20+ NPR member-station republications carrying identical facts — including [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/26/nx-s1-5939965/medical-price-hospital-healthcare-indiana), [KPBS](https://www.kpbs.org/news/health/2026/08/26/states-try-new-tactics-to-bring-down-high-hospital-prices), [Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/npr/2026/08/26/nx-s1-5939965/states-try-new-tactics-to-bring-down-high-hospital-prices/), [WFAE](https://www.wfae.org/science-environment/2026-08-26/states-try-new-tactics-to-bring-down-high-hospital-prices), and [STLPR](https://www.stlpr.org/npr/2026-08-26/states-try-new-tactics-to-bring-down-high-hospital-prices) — two independent research passes surfaced this story via different search paths and reached the same facts, which further increases confidence it's real and correctly dated.
- **Methodology/sample:** Journalism, not a peer-reviewed dataset — treat the specific figures as NPR-reported pending primary-source confirmation (WebFetch to npr.org was blocked, so the underlying Oregon study/methodology could not be independently pulled). The story synthesizes Indiana's new state law alongside earlier state-employee-plan price-cap experiments in Oregon, Montana, and North Carolina.
- **The number:** Indiana's HEA 1004 direct-employer-contracting mandate goes into full effect **September 1, 2026**, requiring **75 hospitals** to offer employers pricing at or below a threshold set as a multiple of Medicare rates (background cited: a 2017 RAND study found some large Indiana hospitals charging 3–4x Medicare). Oregon — the earliest state to test this approach via its own state employee health plan — after **two years** under its price cap saw **outpatient prices per procedure fall 25%** and the state **save over $107 million**. The story also surfaces the standard counter-argument: economist Tony Lo Sasso (University of Wisconsin) notes some Oregon hospitals that had been pricing *below* the cap **raised prices up to it** — a rate-compression effect that doesn't address the underlying driver of provider-market competition.
- **Why it matters:** This is national mainstream-media validation — not a trade-press or consultancy release — that a Medicare-multiple price-cap/RBP mechanism produces measurable, citable employer savings (Oregon's $107M/25% figures) at exactly the moment Indiana's standalone-hospital compliance deadline (5 days out) turns the same mechanism into state law for private employers. It also hands this project a ready-made, honest answer to the most common objection to RBP/direct-contracting pitches — the "hospitals just raise prices up to the cap" critique — sourced to an independent academic rather than an industry advocate, which makes it more credible to cite than a vendor rebuttal.
- **Social angle:** A before/after Oregon chart ("2 years under a price cap: outpatient prices −25%, $107M saved") paired with a callout box for the Lo Sasso caveat framed as "the honest tradeoff" — positions the post as balanced/credible rather than a sales pitch, then lands the Indiana Sept 1 deadline as the "this is happening now" hook. Works as a LinkedIn post today and sets up a natural follow-up post on Sept 1–2 once Indiana's law is actually in effect.
- **Direct-contracting/RBP tie-in flag:** Yes — explicitly and unusually directly. This is a real-world, multi-state case study of the Medicare-multiple pricing mechanism this project promotes, with both a strong result (Oregon) and a fair-minded caveat (price convergence toward the ceiling) in the same story — rare to get both in one citable source.
