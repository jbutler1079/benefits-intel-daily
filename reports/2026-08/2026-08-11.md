### FDA approves $450K first-in-class viral immunotherapy — a live case study in specialty-drug stop-loss exposure
- **Source:** FDA, accelerated approval of Tudriqev (vusolimogene oderparepvec-wtpg, Replimune Inc.), Aug 6, 2026, in combination with nivolumab for treatment-resistant advanced cutaneous melanoma.
- **Methodology/sample:** Pivotal trial data — objective response rate 24.2%, median duration of response 14.1 months. First oncolytic HSV-1 viral immunotherapy approved in the US since Imlygic (2015); approved on a third FDA review cycle.
- **The number:** List price of **$450,000 per full course of treatment** (pre-rebate/discount).
- **Why it matters:** This is exactly the kind of single high-cost claim driving the stop-loss premium trend already tracked in this project (Segal's 12.7% average premium increase; Sun Life's 29%/47% jumps in $1M+/$3M+ claims). A named, dated, six-figure drug launch makes the abstract "specialty drugs are blowing up stop-loss" argument concrete and citable.
- **Social angle:** Single-stat graphic — "$450,000. One drug, one course, one claim that could blow through your specific-stop-loss attachment point." Pairs well with the existing stop-loss premium chart from prior reports.

### Drug Channels: Medicare's drug-spending share is rising as private insurers' share shrinks — employers are the fixed point absorbing the difference
- **Source:** Adam Fein / Drug Channels Institute, "Who Will Pay for Prescription Drugs in 2034? Medicare's Surprising Rise in the Latest Government Forecasts," early August 2026, analyzing new CMS Office of the Actuary National Health Expenditure projections.
- **Methodology/sample:** Analysis of CMS NHE actuarial forecasts through 2034.
- **The number:** Outpatient Rx's share of total Medicare spending projected to rise from ~13% (pre-2022) to ~16% for 2026–2029 (GLP-1/diabetes-obesity driven); private insurance's share of total US drug spending was ~37% in 2024 and is projected to *decline* through 2034, while CMS assumes employer-sponsored enrollment stays essentially flat (178.6M in 2024 → 178.5M in 2034).
- **Why it matters:** The national forecast confirms self-funded employer plans are structurally on the hook for a shrinking-but-still-massive, still-growing specialty-drug bill even as the overall payer mix shifts toward Medicare — undercuts any assumption that market forces will bring employer drug costs down on their own, and strengthens the case for employers to manage PBM/specialty spend proactively now.
- **Social angle:** Line chart of private-insurance vs. Medicare drug-spending share through 2034 — "The market isn't going to fix this for you" LinkedIn take.

### EBRI: 4 in 10 privately insured adults saw costs rise this year — and most are cutting spending or delaying care in response
- **Source:** EBRI Fast Facts #560, "Higher Health Care Costs Prompt Many to Cut Spending, Reduce Saving, or Delay Care," published Aug 6, 2026, drawing on the 2025 EBRI/Greenwald Research Consumer Engagement in Health Care Survey.
- **Methodology/sample:** Online survey of 2,001 U.S. adults ages 21–64 with private health insurance (1,531 traditional plan, 470 HDHP), fielded Oct–Nov 2025.
- **The number:** Among the 4 in 10 privately-insured enrollees who said costs rose in the past year, **more than half cut discretionary spending**, **1 in 3 had trouble paying other bills**, and **1 in 4 reduced retirement-savings contributions**.
- **Why it matters:** Direct evidence that unmanaged commercial cost trend isn't just a line-item on an employer's income statement — it's measurably eroding employees' financial security (including their retirement savings), which is a recruitment/retention argument for employers to pursue structural cost control (direct contracting, RBP) rather than more cost-shifting via deductibles.
- **Social angle:** Stat card / carousel — "1 in 4 workers cut retirement savings because of health costs" as a talent-retention hook for HR/benefits leader audiences.

### KFF/Peterson-KFF: 2027 ACA Marketplace premiums proposed up a median 15% — second-highest since 2018, insurers cite hospital and drug prices directly
- **Source:** KFF analysis of 276 insurer rate filings across all 50 states + DC, published July 8, 2026, updated Aug 3, 2026 with complete state coverage.
- **Methodology/sample:** Full census of individual-market rate filings, not a sample.
- **The number:** Median proposed 2027 premium increase = **15%** (vs. 18% proposed / 20% finalized for 2026); some outliers far higher (e.g., a 52% request from UnitedHealthcare in New York).
- **Why it matters:** This is the individual/ACA marketplace — a different risk pool than employer-sponsored insurance — but insurers' own rate-filing narratives explicitly cite rising hospital and drug prices and provider consolidation as drivers, corroborating the same root-cause story this project tracks for self-funded ESI plans. Useful as market-pressure backdrop, not a direct self-funded data point — keep distinct in posts from the previously-reported Peterson-KFF small-group SHOP 2027 piece (median 14%), a related but separate product.
- **Social angle:** "Different market, same disease" framing — contrast ACA marketplace/small-group premium spikes against the case for employers who self-fund and control their own cost drivers directly.
