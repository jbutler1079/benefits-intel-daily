### Aon's 2027 trend report: employer health costs projected to rise 9.5% again, pushing average cost above $19,000/employee — the fourth straight year near double digits

- **Source:** Aon plc, "2027 U.S. Health Care Cost Trend Report," press release dated August 20, 2026 ([Aon Mediaroom](https://aon.mediaroom.com/2026-08-20-Aon-U-S-Employer-Health-Care-Costs-Continue-Multi-Year-Climb,-Projected-to-Rise-9-5-in-2027)). Independently corroborated same-day/next-day by [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/us-employer-healthcare-costs-set-141247005.html), [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/aon-us-employer-health-care-costs-continue-multi-year-climb-projected-to-rise-9-5-in-2027--302855864.html), [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4635561-employer-healthcare-costs-rise-95-2027-aon), [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/AON/aon-u-s-employer-health-care-costs-continue-multi-year-climb-2wb1aca0f6sy.html), [GV Wire](https://gvwire.com/2026/08/20/us-employer-healthcare-costs-set-to-rise-9-5-in-2027-aon-says/), and [Hawaii Tribune-Herald](https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/2026/08/21/nation-world-news/us-employer-healthcare-costs-set-to-rise-9-5-in-2027-aon-says/) — seven independent outlets.
- **Methodology/sample:** Aon's estimates draw on data from **more than 1,100 U.S. employers covering 7.9 million employees and $135 billion in 2026 health care spending** — a large, carrier/claims-aggregated benchmark rather than a self-reported employer survey.
- **The number:** Employer health care costs are projected to rise **9.5% in 2027**, pushing average cost above **$19,000 per employee**. For 2026 itself, average employer cost is rising 8.8% to **$14,432/employee**; total plan cost (employer + employee) is up 8.3% to **$17,562**. This is the **fourth consecutive year** of near-double-digit trend — one of the most sustained stretches of health care cost inflation employers have faced in decades.
- **Why it matters:** This is a large-sample, high-credibility, freshly dated data point that directly supports the "costs are structurally unsustainable under a fully-insured/status-quo model" argument central to HPHP's pitch. Cited drivers — rising utilization, growing prevalence of chronic conditions, more high-cost claims, and continued growth in specialty-drug and GLP-1 spend — are the same pressure points that make direct contracting, reference-based pricing, and claims-data ownership increasingly attractive to plan sponsors heading into fall 2026 renewal season.
- **Social angle:** A four-year trend-line chart (2024→2027, approaching/at double digits each year) with the caption "This is the fourth year in a row." Pairs well with the previously-reported Mercer (6.5%/6.7%), WTW (9.2%), and Aon's own prior-year 9.5%/2026 figures as a "three firms, one signal, now confirmed a fourth year running" follow-up post. Also stacks naturally against the Regeneron Pasatru specialty-drug figure from yesterday's report as a "macro trend + micro example" two-post sequence.
- **Caveat — flagging honestly:** This does **not** tie to unbundled/direct-contracting positioning or the TX-APCD data gap specifically; it's a general employer-cost-trend benchmark, not a hospital-pricing or PBM-contracting finding. Including it because it clears the bar on specificity, sample size, dating, and corroboration — not because it's central to HPHP's core direct-contracting pitch. Also note: this is Aon's *next-year* (2027) trend report, distinct from the 2026 Aon trend figures (9.5%/$17,000) already logged as background in this project's August 2 report — not a duplicate.
