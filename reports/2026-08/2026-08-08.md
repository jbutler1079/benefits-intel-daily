# Daily Benefits Intelligence — 2026-08-08

### Texas self-funded employers are pulling back on GLP-1 coverage even as self-funding hits a new high in the state
- **Source:** Lockton, "Texas Employers Are Learning to Spend Smarter on Benefits" — the Texas-specific cut of Lockton's 2026 National Benefits Survey, published June 30, 2026.
- **Methodology/sample:** 2026 Lockton National Benefits Survey, 1,705 aggregated employer responses collected nationally in Jan–Feb 2026 (base survey announced April 16, 2026), with a dedicated Texas cut of 200+ Texas respondents published June 30.
- **The number:** 64% of Texas employers now operate self-funded plans, with fully insured arrangements falling from 41% to 32% year-over-year. But among those self-funded Texas employers, only 20% report covering GLP-1s for weight loss — down from nearly 30% a year ago — and another 4% are considering dropping coverage entirely.
- **Why it matters:** Flag prominently — direct-contracting/self-funded positioning, Texas-specific. This cuts against the national "employers keep expanding GLP-1 coverage" narrative (KFF's 2025 survey put GLP-1-for-weight-loss coverage at 43% among firms with 5,000+ employees, still rising as of last year) and against the scale-driven negotiating story in Wednesday's Bank of America item ($250M/year, 13% of health spend, negotiated directly with manufacturers). Texas self-funded employers — mostly without BofA's scale — appear to be doing the opposite: retreating from GLP-1 coverage rather than negotiating it down. That's a direct illustration of the gap unbundled/direct-contracting or carve-out PBM models could fill for mid-size self-funded Texas plans that lack Fortune-10 leverage.
- **Social angle:** Flag prominently — paired-bar or dual-line chart: Texas self-funded share (rising, 64%) vs. self-funded-employer GLP-1 coverage (falling, 30%→20%), captioned "More Texas employers are self-funding than ever. Fewer of them can afford to cover GLP-1s once they do." Follow-on thread: what a carve-out or direct-contracting pharmacy strategy looks like for a self-funded employer without Fortune-10 scale.

### Insurers and TPAs are already pricing in a 15-year-high 2027 — before most employers have started renewal planning
- **Source:** Segal's 2027 Health Plan Cost Trend Survey (the firm's 30th annual trend survey), results published ~July 28, 2026.
- **Methodology/sample:** Survey of health insurers, managed care organizations, PBMs, and third-party administrators fielded late spring–summer 2026; respondents represent a substantial share of the commercial insured and self-insured markets.
- **The number:** Median projected 2027 medical trend of 9.9% for open-access PPO/POS plans, with prescription drug trend projected at 11.5% — both approaching 15-year highs. Segal attributes the Rx trend largely to GLP-1s expanding beyond diabetes into obesity, cardiovascular risk reduction, sleep apnea, and other emerging indications.
- **Why it matters:** This is a forward look at 2027, distinct from the 2026 trend figures (Mercer 6.5%, Aon 9.5%, WTW 9.2%, PwC 9%) already noted as background in this project's coverage. An 11.5% pharmacy trend — mostly GLP-1-driven — reinforces the case for a pharmacy-specific cost lever (carve-out PBM, drug-specific direct contracting) as employers head into 2027 renewal planning this fall.
- **Social angle:** Line chart of medical vs. Rx trend, 2023–2027, captioned "Insurers are already projecting 2027 — and it's a 15-year high." Timed for renewal-planning season (most employers plan Q4 for the following year).
