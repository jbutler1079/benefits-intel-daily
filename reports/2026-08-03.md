# Daily Benefits Intelligence — 2026-08-03

### Healthcare costs just posted their steepest one-year jump in over a decade — and pharmacy is now the single biggest driver
- **Source:** Milliman, "2026 Milliman Medical Index" (MMI), published May 20, 2026
- **Methodology/sample:** Milliman's annual actuarial model of total cost of care (employer contribution + employee premium share + out-of-pocket) for a hypothetical family of four enrolled in a typical employer-sponsored PPO plan, built from Milliman's proprietary healthcare cost and claims databases
- **The number:** Per-person employer-sponsored healthcare cost rose 7.9% from $7,838 (2025) to $8,460 (2026) — the highest annual increase in more than a decade outside pandemic-era swings. For the MMI's hypothetical family of four, total cost hit $37,824. Pharmacy costs alone rose 14.8%, driven by GLP-1 and other high-cost specialty drugs; pharmacy and outpatient facility costs together account for roughly 69% of this year's total cost growth.
- **Why it matters:** This confirms 2026 is a structural break, not noise — and it names the two cost centers (pharmacy, outpatient) that a hospital-repricing-only strategy won't touch. Self-funded plans need a pharmacy-specific lever alongside facility-price interventions.
- **Social angle:** Simple stat card — "$7,838 → $8,460: the steepest jump in a decade" — paired with a two-slice breakdown (pharmacy/outpatient = 69% of growth) as a LinkedIn carousel.

### A peer-reviewed study just put a $10.8 billion price tag on what carriers are leaving on the table by not going site-neutral
- **Source:** Health Affairs Scholar (Oxford Academic/PMC), "Site-neutral payment for routine services could save commercial purchasers and patients billions" — research supported by Arnold Ventures
- **Methodology/sample:** Modeled the effect of extending Medicare-style site-neutral payment policy (paying the same rate for a routine service regardless of whether it's billed by a hospital outpatient department or an independent physician office) to the commercial insurance market across 48 states and D.C.
- **The number:** Extending site-neutral payment to routine services in the commercial market alone would save an estimated $10.8 billion annually — separate, broader MedPAC-based modeling using 2022 data puts full site-neutral savings across all eligible services as high as $58 billion in a single year.
- **Why it matters:** This is peer-reviewed confirmation that the "same service, different price tag by location" problem CMS is now attacking in Medicare (2026 OPPS rule) is even larger in the commercial book — and it's a price gap that exists independent of which carrier network you're in.
- **Social angle:** Flag prominently — direct-contracting/unbundled-TPA positioning. "$10.8B is sitting in the gap between what a hospital outpatient department bills and what the same exact service costs in an independent office" — strong chart candidate (HOPD vs. office price bar, times national savings estimate).

### CMS more than doubled its price-transparency enforcement pace in 2025 — and the 2026 rule closes the loophole hospitals were using to fudge the numbers
- **Source:** CMS, "CY 2026 OPPS and Ambulatory Surgical Center Final Rule — Hospital Price Transparency Policy Changes" (published Nov. 21, 2025; enforcement effective April 1, 2026); enforcement pace reported by BenefitsPro/Word & Brown, May 14, 2026
- **Methodology/sample:** CMS rulemaking plus CMS's own published civil monetary penalty (CMP) actions against noncompliant hospitals
- **The number:** CMS issued 10 civil monetary penalties against hospitals in 2025 — more than double the prior year's enforcement pace. The CY2026 rule now requires hospitals to publish actual dollar amounts drawn from electronic remittance advice data in place of the "estimated allowed amount" hospitals previously listed when a payer-specific rate was expressed as a percentage or algorithm.
- **Why it matters:** The transparency data self-funded employers and consultants have been building repricing and direct-contracting analyses on on has had a known garbage-in problem — estimated, algorithm-derived rates instead of real ones. The 2026 rule and the enforcement uptick both point the same direction: the underlying data is about to get materially more reliable, which strengthens (not weakens) the case for pricing strategies built on it.
- **Social angle:** Timeline graphic — CMP count by year — captioned "CMS just doubled down on price transparency enforcement. Here's why your MRF-based repricing analysis is about to get more accurate."

### Large employers now say healthcare costs are running 62% above where they were nine years ago — and this year's median trend estimate is 9%
- **Source:** Business Group on Health, "2026 Employer Health Care Strategy Survey" (large-employer coalition survey, published 2026)
- **Methodology/sample:** Annual survey of Business Group on Health's member employers — large, self-funded employers that collectively cover millions of workers and dependents
- **The number:** Employers project a median 2026 health care cost trend of 9%, falling to 7.6% if they implement plan-design changes. On a compounded basis, member employers estimate 2026 costs are running 62% higher than 2017 levels — this is 2026 being described as the fourth consecutive year of elevated cost growth following a decade that averaged roughly 3% annually.
- **Why it matters:** The 62% cumulative figure reframes "another bad trend year" as a near-decade-long structural shift, which is the more persuasive frame for employers deciding whether to make a one-time plan-design tweak or restructure how they buy care entirely.
- **Social angle:** Single stark stat: "Healthcare costs are up 62% since 2017. Trend used to run ~3%/year. It's now 9%." Good standalone LinkedIn/X post, no chart needed.
