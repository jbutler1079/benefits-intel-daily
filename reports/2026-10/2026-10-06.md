### HHS/DOL/Treasury finalize updated Transparency in Coverage rule — monthly MRF posting replaced by quarterly cadence, phone-based cost estimates now required

- **Source:** HHS / CMS / DOL / Treasury, Transparency in Coverage Final Rule, published October 5, 2026 (Federal Register); HHS press release same date
- **Methodology/sample:** Federal rulemaking — applies to all non-grandfathered group health plans and health insurance issuers subject to TiC (i.e., essentially all self-funded ERISA plans)
- **The number:** MRF update cadence moves from **monthly to quarterly**; cost-sharing information must now be available by **phone** (in addition to the online price-comparison tool and paper-on-request); effective 60 days after Federal Register publication, with implementation milestones through late 2027
- **Why it matters:** For self-funded plan sponsors and their TPAs, this is a compliance action item — not background. The employer remains legally responsible for MRF accuracy even when a TPA hosts the files. The rule also adds required data elements (provider network identifiers, expanded out-of-network pricing context) and a standardized homepage-linking requirement, meaning TPAs and vendors need to update hosting and schema workflows. The phone-access requirement closes a gap the original rule left for employees without online access.
- **Social angle:** LinkedIn post — "New federal rule dropped yesterday. If you sponsor a self-funded health plan, your TPA's MRF posting just changed. Here's what you need to confirm by [effective date]." Tag: compliance, TiC, self-funded, CAA, price transparency. Thread format: (1) What changed, (2) What employers owe vs. what TPAs owe, (3) The one question to ask your TPA today.

**🔗 Unbundled/direct-contracting flag:** TiC MRF data is the raw input for any reference-based pricing or direct-contracting strategy. Plans that build direct contracts need accurate, current MRF feeds. The quarterly-vs-monthly tradeoff reduces burden but means price data could be up to 90 days stale — worth flagging in vendor agreements.

### CMS hospital price transparency enforcement: 500+ warnings issued since April 2026; only 28 hospitals ever fined since 2021

- **Source:** CMS Hospital Price Transparency Enforcement Activities and Outcomes dataset (data.cms.gov, monthly-updated); Becker's Hospital Review summary, July 2026
- **Methodology/sample:** CMS compliance review database — all U.S. hospitals subject to hospital price transparency rule; enforcement began Jan 2021, new requirements effective/enforced April 1, 2026
- **The number:** **500+ hospital warning notices** issued since April 1, 2026; only **28 hospitals total fined** since 2021. Most 2026 citations were for technical/formatting errors. One hospital (Pinnacle Hospital, Indiana) fined twice in 2026 — $51,615 for 155 days of noncompliance, then $40,356 for another 118 days.
- **Why it matters:** Enforcement is accelerating (500 warnings in ~6 months) but financial penalties remain rare — a gap that many hospitals appear willing to exploit. For employers and plan sponsors relying on hospital MRFs for claims benchmarking or direct-contract negotiations, file quality is still inconsistent. The new 2026 requirements (median and percentile allowed amounts, CEO attestation, 835-ERА-based actuals) are designed to close the accuracy gap, but compliance is still being phased in.
- **Social angle:** LinkedIn take — "CMS sent 500+ warning letters to hospitals in six months. Only 28 have ever been fined in 5 years. What does that tell you about hospital price transparency enforcement?" Chart: warnings vs. fines since 2021.

**🔗 TX-APCD / direct-contracting flag:** In Texas, where APCD data is incomplete, employers trying to benchmark hospital prices are especially dependent on MRF quality. Inconsistent hospital MRF compliance is a concrete reason to pair MRF analysis with claims-level data or reference-based pricing benchmarks rather than treating MRFs as ground truth.
