### Small self-funded employers are leaving the Big 3 PBMs fastest: 69.7% → 43.8% in one year
- **Source:** National Alliance of Healthcare Purchaser Coalitions, *2026 Pulse of the Purchaser*, released Aug 11, 2026 (via HCA Mag: https://www.hcamag.com/us/specialization/benefits/pharmacy-benefit-managers-lose-ground-as-employers-press-for-reform/592706)
- **Methodology/sample:** 408 employers/purchasers surveyed May–June 2026; self-reported PBM use among self-funded employers. Survey, not claims data.
- **The number:** Big 3 (Caremark, Express Scripts, Optum Rx) share among self-funded employers fell 63.4% → 54.3% (2025→2026); among employers <1,000 employees, 69.7% → 43.8%. 56% of Big 3 clients are considering a switch within 1–3 years.
- **Why it matters:** The unbundled/transparent PBM model is moving from niche to mainstream among small self-funded groups, the segment HPHP targets. Big 3 are responding (Optum Rx fee-based model announced May 2026; Express Scripts rebate-free pass-through by 2028; Caremark signaling cost-plus), which validates the contracting critique but narrows the differentiation to enforcement, audit rights and data ownership.
- **Social angle:** Two-bar chart (all vs. <1,000 employees, 2025 vs. 2026) + LinkedIn take: "The 'nobody gets fired for choosing the Big 3' era is ending for small employers — a 26-point drop in a year."

### Employers project 2027 health cost growth of 8–9%, highest in ~20 years — and have under-projected three years running
- **Source:** Business Group on Health, 2027 Employer Healthcare Strategy Survey (Healthcare Dive, Aug 26, 2026: https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/employers-2027-health-costs-bgh-survey/828734/); corroborated by Marsh 2026 National Survey of Employer-Sponsored Health Plans (preliminary, responses through Aug 10; via Fierce Healthcare) and Aon (9.5%, August).
- **Methodology/sample:** BGH: 127 large employers covering ~8.7M people. Marsh preliminary figures not verified from primary text (fetch blocked).
- **The number:** BGH median 9.2% projected increase for 2027 (~8% after plan changes); pharmacy +12% in both 2026 and 2027; 70% cite cancer as a top cost driver (up from 58%); employers say No Surprises Act IDR adds ~2% to trend; employers have underestimated actual spend three straight years, with the 2025 miss the largest in survey history except 2020. Marsh: 8.2% (largest since 2003), ~11% before cost-reduction actions, 59% of employers plan changes.
- **Why it matters:** Plan-design shifts and cost-sharing alone are not bending trend; the named drivers (hospital consolidation/facility fees, specialty Rx, GLP-1s) are price/contracting problems that benefit from direct contracting, reference-based pricing and transparent PBM terms. Budgets built on last year's trend are likely short again.
- **Social angle:** Single-stat card ("Employers have under-projected health costs 3 years straight") plus short thread on the three drivers and which are contractable.
