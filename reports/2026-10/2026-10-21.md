### Brand-name drugs were 14% of prescriptions but 86% of gross drug spending; gross Rx prices +33% (2019–2023)
- **Source:** Health Care Cost Institute, 2023 Health Care Cost and Utilization Report (released Oct 13, 2026; via HCA Mag — primary report not read)
- **Methodology/sample:** Commercial claims 2019–2023, under-65 employer-sponsored insurance, weighted to the national ESI population (~half the US) by age, sex, geography.
- **The number:** Brand = 14% of scripts, 86% of gross drug spend. Gross Rx prices +33%; generic prices −14%. Prices for administered drugs billed in professional services +54%.
- **Why it matters:** Pharmacy cost is concentrated in a small slice of scripts, so plan design and PBM contracting around brand/specialty net price matter far more than generic dispensing fees. Administered (medical-benefit) drugs are a cost center many pharmacy-only reviews miss. *Ties to PBM pass-through / unbundled positioning; moderate.*
- **Social angle:** Two-bar chart "14% of prescriptions → 86% of spend," caption on where to negotiate.

### GLP-1s: gross spend per person $45 → $197, but net only $20 → $93 — rebates can exceed 50% of gross cost
- **Source:** HCCI, 2023 Health Care Cost and Utilization Report (via HCA Mag, Oct 2026 — primary not read)
- **Methodology/sample:** Same as above; GLP-1 months' supply per 1,000 enrollees rose 54 → 204.
- **The number:** Gross $197 vs net $93 per person per year in 2023 (~12% of gross drug spend); use up ~3.8x.
- **Why it matters:** The gap between gross and net is the PBM-controlled number. Employers can't evaluate GLP-1 coverage decisions without knowing what share of rebates actually reaches the plan. Data is 2023, so current exposure is likely higher. *Ties to PBM rebate transparency / audit rights.*
- **Social angle:** LinkedIn take: "Your GLP-1 cost has two numbers. Do you know which one your plan pays?"

### Avoidable site-of-care spending: $12.7B a year across employment-based plans (~$101 per covered member)
- **Source:** EBRI estimate, cited in HCA Mag coverage of the HCCI report (Oct 2026); EBRI primary study not located
- **Methodology/sample:** Not stated in secondary coverage.
- **The number:** $12.7B avoidable annual spend, ~$101 per member per year. HCCI reportedly finds the hospital-outpatient vs physician-office price gap widened 2019–2023 amid practice acquisitions (no dollar figure given).
- **Why it matters:** Site-of-care steering is a concrete direct-contracting lever for self-funded plans; per-member figure makes it easy to size for a group. *Ties to direct-contracting positioning. Low confidence until EBRI source is confirmed.*
