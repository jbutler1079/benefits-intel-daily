### Prices, not utilization, drove 2019–2023 employer spending growth: prices +17.7% vs. utilization +1.8%
- **Source:** Health Care Cost Institute (HCCI), Health Care Cost and Utilization Report, released Oct 13, 2026 (figures via [Managed Healthcare Executive coverage](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/katie-martin-still-prices-matter-most-u-s-employer-healthcare-spending); HCCI's own page was not retrievable today — verify against the primary report before posting).
- **Methodology/sample:** Commercial claims for ~30 million people under 65 with employer-sponsored insurance, 2019–2023.
- **The number:** Per-person spending up 18.8% (+$1,068); prices up 17.7%; utilization up just 1.8%. Hospital care (inpatient + outpatient) is 46% of dollars spent. Inpatient use fell, i.e., care is moving out of the highest-price settings, yet spending still rose.
- **Why it matters:** Employers are told cost growth is about "overuse" and plan-design/cost-sharing; this says ~90%+ of growth is unit price. That supports price-side levers (direct contracting, reference-based pricing, unbundled TPA with claims access) over demand-side tweaks. Not specifically TX-APCD, but reinforces why price-level data access matters.
- **Social angle:** Simple two-bar chart (price +17.7% vs. utilization +1.8%) with caption "Employers' costs aren't rising because people use more care." LinkedIn take tying to direct contracting.
