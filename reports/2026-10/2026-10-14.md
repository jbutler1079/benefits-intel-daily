### Reference-based pricing paid hospitals 122% of Medicare inpatient vs. 240% for other commercial payers — est. 56.5% savings
- **Source:** Murray & Whaley (Brown University), "Commercial prices relative to Medicare were lower under an employer-based reference pricing program," *Health Affairs Scholar* 2026;4(7):qxag156, online June 27, 2026 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334083/)
- **Methodology/sample:** Imagine360 reference-based-pricing plan claims (2024): 188,657 claims at 2,684 hospitals in 49 states + DC (excl. Maryland). Compared against commercial prices from RAND Round 5.1 (2022 data, assumed similar in 2024). Descriptive, cross-sectional; excludes program administration costs; no access/quality outcomes.
- **The number:** Inpatient 122% of Medicare vs. 240% commercial (−118 pts); outpatient 149% vs. 311% (−162 pts). Estimated savings ~$417M, or 56.5% of spend at commercial rates ($232M outpatient, $184M inpatient).
- **Why it matters:** Claims-level evidence across ~2,700 hospitals that employer-directed pricing, not just network discounts, can cut hospital spend by roughly half. Directly supports unbundled/direct-contracting positioning for self-funded employers. Caveat for fair use: no admin-cost, access, or balance-billing analysis.
