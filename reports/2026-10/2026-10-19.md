### 94% of local PBM markets are "highly concentrated" (up from 82% in 2022); top-4 PBMs now hold 75% nationally
- **Source:** American Medical Association, Policy Research Perspectives, "Competition in pharmacy benefit manager markets and vertical integration with insurers: 2026 Update," published Oct. 8, 2026 — https://www.ama-assn.org/about/ama-research/94-local-pbm-markets-highly-concentrated-says-ama-report
- **Methodology/sample:** Prescription-drug plan enrollment data for 2022 and 2024, assessed against federal antitrust concentration thresholds at national and local-market level. Market count and HHI not stated on the summary page (full report not reviewed). AMA is an interested party in provider/payer disputes; treat as secondary to FTC/GAO data.
- **The number:** 94% of local PBM markets highly concentrated in 2024 (82% in 2022). Top 4 PBMs = 75% national share (70% in 2022): OptumRx 23%, Express Scripts 23%, CVS/Caremark 18%, Prime 11%. All 10 largest PBMs share ownership with an insurer; 67% of commercial enrollees (73% of Part D) were in a vertically integrated plan.
- **Why it matters:** Self-funded employers with a carrier-aligned PBM are buying from a conflicted, concentrated market where there is little local competition to discipline pricing. This supports unbundled/carve-out pharmacy contracting with pass-through terms and audit rights. Note Express Scripts' share jumped 6 points in two years, consistent with the previously reported flight of small self-funded employers from the Big 3.
- **Social angle:** LinkedIn take or simple bar chart: "75% of PBM enrollment sits with 4 companies — and every top-10 PBM is tied to an insurer. Who is auditing your pharmacy spend?" Flag: **unbundled/direct-contracting positioning**.
