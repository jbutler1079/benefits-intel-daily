# Daily Research — 2026-10-30

**Summary:** Major PBM policy event today: DOL finalized its PBM fee-disclosure rule. This is a material update for self-funded employer fiduciaries and should be treated as a top-of-funnel content item.

### DOL finalizes PBM fee disclosure for large self-funded plans (RIN 1210-AB37)
- **Source:** U.S. Department of Labor, EBSA final rule, *Improving Transparency into Pharmacy Benefit Manager Fee Disclosure* (Federal Register Vol. 91, No. 192, Oct. 6, 2026, pp. 63748-63867, Doc. 2026-20447); DOL ERISA rule page (Oct. 2026).
- **Methodology/sample:** Final federal rulemaking under ERISA service-provider disclosure requirements (29 CFR Part 2550.408b-2) governing PBM compensation disclosures to plan fiduciaries.
- **The number:** Rule applies when expected PBM compensation is **$1,000+**; scope targets large plans (generally **100+** participants/employees); disclosures required before contract/renewal and updated at least **semiannually** (or more frequently if requested); reported policy estimate indicates up to **$22B** in prescription-cost reduction over **10 years**.
- **Why it matters:** Self-funded employers now have a clearer fiduciary basis to demand full spread/rebate/affiliate compensation visibility and to renegotiate PBM terms using enforceable disclosure mechanics rather than contractual goodwill.
- **Social angle:** LinkedIn doc post: “What the PBM Final Rule changes in 4 bullets” + one slide on fiduciary checklist (data ownership, spread disclosure, affiliate compensation, audit rights).

**Direct-contracting / unbundled tie (flag):** This rule strengthens the case for pass-through and unbundled PBM procurement by making hidden compensation streams an explicit fiduciary oversight issue.

**TX-APCD tie (flag):** Rule improves contract-level transparency but does not solve state APCD visibility gaps on employer-specific pharmacy net-cost performance, so Texas purchasers still need independent analytics and contract audit rights.
