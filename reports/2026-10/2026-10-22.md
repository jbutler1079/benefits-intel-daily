### 71% of covered workers are in self-funded plans — 85% at large firms, 25% at small firms; 38% of small-firm workers are already in level-funded plans
- **Source:** KFF, 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey (28th annual), Oct 22, 2026
- **Methodology/sample:** 1,937 non-federal public/private firms with 10+ workers (1,668 offering benefits); drawn from 35,781 firms, 11% response rate; Davis Research fieldwork Jan–July 2026; ~10% of observations imputed.
- **The number:** 71% of covered workers self-funded (85% large / 25% small); 38% of small-firm covered workers in level-funded plans.
- **Why it matters:** Self-funding is the norm for large employers, and level-funding is the on-ramp for small ones, so the pool of groups that could adopt direct contracting or unbundled TPA models is far larger than "self-funded" alone suggests. *Ties to unbundled/direct-contracting positioning; high.*
- **Social angle:** Chart: self-funded share by firm size, with the level-funded slice highlighted. Caption: "Most covered workers are already in self-funded plans. Are the people running yours using that freedom?"

### Small-firm workers pay 37% of the family premium ($9,687) vs. 25% ($6,840) at large firms; 55% of small-firm workers face a $2,000+ deductible
- **Source:** KFF, 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey, Oct 22, 2026
- **Methodology/sample:** As above; dollar figures weighted by covered workers.
- **The number:** Family worker contribution $9,687 (firms 10–199) vs. $6,840 (larger). 31% of small-firm covered workers face family contributions of $12,000+ (13% overall). 38% of all covered workers have a single deductible of $2,000+, up from 34%; 55% at small firms vs. 33% at large. Average single deductible $1,955 overall.
- **Why it matters:** Cost is shifting to workers fastest where employers have the least purchasing leverage. A plan that lowers the underlying price (reference-based pricing, direct contracts) is an alternative to raising employee contributions or deductibles. *Ties to direct-contracting positioning; moderate.*
- **Social angle:** Side-by-side bars of small vs. large firm worker contribution; LinkedIn take on "cost shifting is a leverage problem, not a benefit-design problem."

### Family premium up 28% over five years — faster than wages (26.9%) and inflation (24.4%); worker share of family premium rose ~9% to $7,471 this year
- **Source:** KFF, 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey, Oct 22, 2026
- **Methodology/sample:** As above.
- **The number:** Family premium $28,303 (+5% in 2026); single $9,804 (+5%). 5-year change +28% vs. wages +26.9% and inflation +24.4%. This year wages +3.7%, inflation +2.7%. Worker family contribution $7,471 (~27% of premium; single $1,672, ~18%).
- **Why it matters:** The headline 5% understates the burden: workers' own contribution grew ~9% (vs. 3.7% wage growth), and 2027 projections from consultants approach double digits. Only 17% of employers are "very satisfied" with cost of care; 29% are dissatisfied. *Context stat; no specific TX-APCD or direct-contracting tie.*
- **Social angle:** Single-stat card: "Worker contribution +9%. Wages +3.7%."

### Only 18% of firms with 200+ workers cover GLP-1s for weight loss — and 29% of those say the impact on drug spend is "significant"
- **Source:** KFF, 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey, Oct 22, 2026
- **Methodology/sample:** As above; firms with 200+ workers.
- **The number:** 18% overall (19% last year); 16% at 200–999 workers, 29% at 1,000–4,999, 33% at 5,000+. 29% of covering firms call the effect on Rx spending significant. (KFF Health News phrases the 29% as "all surveyed companies"; the KFF page says covering firms — verify wording before posting.)
- **Why it matters:** Coverage is flat year over year despite cost pressure, and is concentrated in the largest employers. Smaller self-funded plans are making the coverage decision with less PBM net-price visibility. *Ties to PBM transparency; moderate.*
- **Social angle:** Chart of GLP-1 coverage by firm size; thread on what to ask a PBM before deciding.
