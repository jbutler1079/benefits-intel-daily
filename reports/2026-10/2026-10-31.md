# Daily Research — 2026-10-31

The KFF 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey (released 2026-10-22) is the one new primary dataset found. It was listed as an upcoming release in earlier reports. Other searches (RAND/commercial-vs-Medicare pricing, PBM/employer studies, HCCI/Health Affairs) turned up only previously covered or older material, so nothing else is added. Findings below come from the KFF survey page and its Quick Insight summary.

### Worker share of family premiums rose 9% ($621) to $7,471, while the total premium rose 5%
- **Source:** KFF, 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey (28th edition), released 2026-10-22
- **Methodology/sample:** Annual survey of public and private firms, fielded Jan–Jul 2026. 1,937 firms completed the full survey; 35,781 were sampled; 11% response rate.
- **The number:** Average family premium $28,303 (+5%, +$1,310 vs $26,993). Worker contribution $7,471 (+9%). Single premium $9,804. Wages rose 3.7% and inflation 2.7%. Over five years, family premiums are up 28% against 26.9% for wages.
- **Why it matters:** Cost growth is moving to workers faster than it is growing in total. 18% of all firms raised worker premium shares, and 66% of firms with 5,000+ employees did. Employers who do not change how they buy care are mostly shifting cost, not reducing it.
- **Social angle:** Simple bar or line chart of premium vs wage vs inflation growth, with a one-line LinkedIn take on cost shifting versus cost reduction.

### Small-firm workers pay $9,687 for family coverage vs $6,840 at large firms, about 37% of premium vs 25%
- **Source:** KFF, 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey, 2026-10-22
- **Methodology/sample:** Same as above; comparison of firms with 10–199 workers against larger firms.
- **The number:** Small-firm worker contribution $9,687 vs $6,840 at larger firms. Average single deductible $2,624 at smaller firms vs $1,772 at larger firms. 55% of small-firm workers have a $2,000+ deductible, vs 33% at large firms. Overall, 38% of covered workers have a $2,000+ deductible (up from 34%).
- **Why it matters:** The small and mid-size employers that HPHP-style direct-contracting models target carry the heaviest worker burden. Small groups also lack the leverage large employers have, which makes the case for alternative purchasing models.
- **Social angle:** Side-by-side small vs large firm graphic ("same coverage, $2,847 more out of the worker's pocket"). The $2,847 is our subtraction ($9,687 − $6,840).

### 71% of covered workers are now in self-funded plans (up from 67%): 85% at large firms, 25% at small firms, and 38% of small-firm workers are in level-funded plans
- **Source:** KFF, 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey, 2026-10-22
- **Methodology/sample:** Same as above, weighted to covered workers.
- **The number:** 71% self-funded in 2026 vs 67% in 2025. Small firms: 25% self-funded, 38% of covered workers level-funded. KFF does not report a stop-loss enrollment or cost figure.
- **Why it matters:** Self-funding is now the default, and level-funding is the entry point for small employers. Plan sponsors of these plans carry fiduciary duties over PBM and hospital pricing, which is where unbundled TPA and direct-contracting positioning applies. **Flag: unbundled/direct-contracting positioning.** The KFF data shows adoption but nothing about prices paid, which is the data gap TX-APCD and transparency files are meant to fill.
- **Social angle:** LinkedIn take: "Most covered workers are already in self-funded plans, but most employers still can't see what they are paying." Use the 71% figure as the hook.

## Caveats
- KFF changed plan-type classification in 2026 (HRA-linked high-deductible plans reclassified; HSA-qualified plans a separate type), so some year-over-year plan-type comparisons may not be like-for-like. Premium and contribution figures are unaffected as reported.
- I read only the first ~100,000 of ~355,000 characters of the KFF web page; the full PDF was too large to fetch. Verify figures against the full report before publishing.
- Related but not reported as findings: GLP-1 weight-loss coverage among firms with 200+ workers is 18% (19% in 2025), which looks like a plateau.
