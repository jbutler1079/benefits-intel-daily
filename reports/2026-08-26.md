### WTW: employers face an 11.1% healthcare cost jump in 2027 — the steepest in nearly two decades — but 56% won't take the "disruptive" actions needed to blunt it

- **Source:** Willis Towers Watson (WTW), 2026 Best Practices in Healthcare Survey, press coverage dated August 20–25, 2026. Independently corroborated across [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/benefits/wtw-projects-the-steepest-healthcare-cost-increase-in-nearly-two-decades-587155.aspx), [CFO Dive](https://www.cfodive.com/news/firms-may-shrink-healthcare-plans-avoid-cost-bump-wtw-says/828541/), [The Fiscal Times](https://www.thefiscaltimes.com/2026/08/20/Employers-Expect-Healthcare-Costs-Rise-111-2027-Survey), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/employers-expect-healthcare-costs-rise-205348978.html), [FierceHealthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/employers-brace-another-year-healthcare-cost-increases), [Advisory.com](https://www.advisory.com/daily-briefing/2026/08/25/insurance-costs), and [jrreport.wordandbrown.com](https://jrreport.wordandbrown.com/2026/08/25/workers-are-paying-more-for-employer-healthcare-plans-and-2027-could-be-worse/) — seven independent outlets, two dated as late as Aug 25 (within this window).
- **Methodology/sample:** 471 U.S. employers, representing 7 million employees, at companies ranging from 100 to 25,000+ workers. Fielded June 22 – August 7, 2026; full results due in September (this is a preliminary/topline release).
- **The number:** Employers project healthcare costs will rise **11.1% in 2027 if no corrective action is taken** — the steepest single-year projected increase in nearly two decades. Plan redesign could cut that to **9.7%**, but the survey found most employers are unwilling or unready to make the structural changes required: **56% said they are unwilling to take "disruptive" actions** — which WTW itself defines as **self-funding transitions, reference-based pricing, direct contracting, and pharmacy carve-outs** — the specific structural moves that shift costs meaningfully rather than at the margins.
- **Why it matters:** This is the third major consultancy in five weeks (after Aon's 9.5% and Business Group on Health's ~9%) to confirm near-record employer cost trend for 2027, and WTW's 11.1% no-action baseline is the highest and most alarming figure yet. More importantly, WTW's own framing draws the exact line this project tracks: the employers who will keep bleeding are the majority (56%) unwilling to adopt self-funding/RBP/direct-contracting/carve-out models, while the minority willing to make structural changes have a credible path to nearly 150 bps of relief. That's a direct, citable articulation of the "disruption gap" that HPHP's go-to-market thesis is built on.
- **Social angle:** A two-bar chart — "11.1% if you do nothing" vs. "9.7% if you redesign" — captioned "That gap is worth acting on." Pairs as a direct sequel to the Aon/Mercer/WTW "three firms, one signal" thread from the Aug 21 report, but this is the first of the three to explicitly name self-funding, RBP, direct contracting, and pharmacy carve-outs as the mechanism for closing the gap — makes it the strongest of the three for a direct LinkedIn thread aimed at plan sponsors.
- **Direct-contracting/RBP tie-in flag:** Yes — explicitly. This is one of the more directly on-thesis findings logged this month; WTW is not just reporting a cost trend, it is naming the exact unbundled/direct-contracting toolkit as the differentiator between employers who solve the problem and the majority who don't.
