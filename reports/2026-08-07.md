# Daily Benefits Intelligence — 2026-08-07

### Texas leads the nation in CMS hospital price-transparency violations (42 warning letters) — and no one has published a Texas-specific breakdown yet
- **Source:** CMS Hospital Price Transparency enforcement actions, April–early June 2026 warning-letter cycle (rule updated 2024, enforcement of updated requirements began April 1, 2026); rolling metro-level breakdowns published by insurer-backed watchdog site Hospital Watch through late July 2026 (Chicago, Wisconsin, North Carolina, Minnesota, Indianapolis — no Texas installment yet).
- **Methodology/sample:** CMS's own compliance-review/warning-letter process, covering 519 hospitals nationwide across every state but Alaska. Hospital Watch's metro reports repackage the same CMS list by geography — their framing is advocacy (BCBS/AHIP-affiliated), not neutral, but the underlying counts trace directly to CMS.
- **The number:** 519 hospitals received noncompliance warning letters between April and early June 2026. Texas hospitals received the most of any state — 42 — ahead of California (38) and Indiana (34). Named Texas recipients include Baptist Medical Center San Antonio (1,585 beds) and UT MD Anderson Cancer Center Houston. CMS has issued 28 civil monetary penalties total since the rule took effect in 2021, with fines scaling by bed count up to roughly $5,500/day (~$2M+/year exposure for large systems).
- **Why it matters:** Flag prominently — direct-contracting positioning and TX-APCD data gap. Texas leads the country in price-transparency noncompliance, yet unlike Indianapolis, Chicago, Wisconsin, Minnesota, and North Carolina — which have each gotten a dedicated local watchdog breakdown — no metro-level Texas report exists yet. That's a live content gap: HPHP/Butler Benefits could credibly be first to pair the CMS TX=42 figure with TX-APCD claims data to name which Texas systems are both least transparent and highest-priced, reinforcing the "you can't negotiate against a price you can't see" direct-contracting thesis.
- **Social angle:** Flag prominently — state-comparison bar chart (TX 42 / CA 38 / IN 34 / next several states), captioned "Texas has the most price-transparency violations in America. Nobody's mapped which of these systems are also charging self-funded plans the most — yet." Set up a follow-on post pulling TX-APCD data against the named noncompliant systems.

### Bank of America is spending $250M+/year on GLP-1s — 13% of its entire health care budget on one drug class
- **Source:** CNBC, on-record interview with Bank of America CEO Brian Moynihan, published Aug. 5, 2026.
- **Methodology/sample:** Single self-insured employer's own disclosure (~213,000 employees), not a survey or study — but a specific, named, on-the-record figure rather than an industry-wide estimate.
- **The number:** BofA now spends more than $250 million per year on GLP-1 coverage, up from $0 roughly five years ago; GLP-1s account for approximately 13% of the company's total health care spend. Moynihan cited using BofA's scale to negotiate directly with manufacturers and PBMs on price.
- **Why it matters:** This is the kind of concrete number the usual industry-wide percentages (e.g., "GLP-1s are ~11% of claims") stay abstract about, and it illustrates direct exposure: pharmacy cost concentration in a single drug class is now large enough that even a Fortune 10 self-insured plan sponsor is using its scale to negotiate directly — a real-world validation of the direct-contracting logic, just at a scale most self-funded employers don't have. Useful contrast piece: what does a mid-size Texas employer do about the same cost pressure without BofA's leverage (unbundled TPA + carve-out PBM, reference-based pricing, direct contracting)?
- **Social angle:** Single-stat card — "One bank. One drug class. $250,000,000/year — 13% of its total health budget." — paired with a follow-up thread on GLP-1 cost-management options for employers who don't have Fortune-10 negotiating scale.
