### Hospital costs are the only part of medical inflation running hot — up 5.2% y/y while drugs and physician services cool off

- **Source:** Bureau of Labor Statistics, Consumer Price Index Summary —
  August 2026 (release USDL-26-1496), published September 11, 2026.
- **Methodology/sample:** BLS's standard monthly CPI-U survey (national
  retail price sample across the medical care spending category);
  seasonally adjusted month-over-month change and not-seasonally-adjusted
  12-month change, both ending August 2026.
- **The number:** **Hospital services rose 5.2% y/y** (0.0% m/m SA) —
  more than double the **2.5% y/y** rise for medical care services
  overall, and well above the **2.0% y/y** for physicians' services.
  Medical care commodities (chiefly prescription drugs) actually **fell
  2.7% y/y**. Headline CPI-U rose 3.4% y/y over the same period — meaning
  hospital inflation is running at roughly 1.5x the economy-wide rate
  while drugs are net deflationary year-over-year.
- **Why it matters:** For self-funded plans, hospital facility costs —
  not drugs, not physician fees — are the line item outrunning general
  inflation. That's a direct argument for site-of-care steering,
  reference-based pricing on facility claims, and direct hospital
  contracting, rather than PBM-focused cost containment as the primary
  lever right now.
- **Social angle:** Simple four-bar comparison chart: headline CPI 3.4%
  vs. hospital services 5.2% vs. physicians' services 2.0% vs. drugs
  −2.7% — the "who's actually driving your trend" chart.
- **Direct-contracting/TX-APCD tie-in flag:** Direct — hospital facility
  costs, the exact target of direct-contracting and reference-based
  pricing models, are the one medical-care category still running hot.
  Reinforces the pitch that unbundled hospital contracting matters more
  than PBM-only interventions at the moment.
