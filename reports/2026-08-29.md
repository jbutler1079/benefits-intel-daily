# Daily Healthcare Benefits Research — 2026-08-29

### 1. Texas hospitals need as little as 85% of Medicare to break even — but employers are paying 163% of Medicare
- **Source:** National Academy for State Health Policy (NASHP), "Texas House Select Committee on Health Affordability: Hospital Prices," presented by Maureen Hensley-Quinn, April 30, 2026 (built on NASHP's Hospital Cost Tool / CMS Medicare Cost Reports, with RAND employer-claims price data layered in). https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/handouts/C0592026043009001/acd8517c-8dcf-4b37-89ff-725a5dc8ff9f.PDF (slides 6–7, 14, 20)
- **Methodology/sample:** NASHP's Hospital Cost Tool computes each hospital's "commercial breakeven" — the % of Medicare needed to cover total hospital expenses — from ~5,000 hospitals' 2011–2024 Medicare Cost Report data, paired with RAND's employer-claims-based price data for the same Texas systems.
- **The number:** Statewide TX median commercial breakeven = 110% of Medicare (national = 112%). For Texas **independent (non-system) hospitals**: median breakeven = 85% of Medicare, while the median RAND price actually paid by employers = 163% of Medicare. Also: 96% of TX hospitals sit in "highly concentrated" markets (HHI >1,800, Yale Health Care Affordability Lab, 2025); system ownership of TX hospitals rose from 53% (2000) to 64% (2025) — 262 hospitals now in 34 systems vs. 148 independents.
- **Why it matters:** A hospital-by-hospital, Texas-specific breakeven-vs-price gap — direct evidence many TX hospitals (especially independents) could be paid far closer to Medicare and remain solvent, which is the core economic case for reference-based pricing and direct/unbundled contracting.
- **Texas APCD / direct-contracting flag:** Yes — explicitly Texas-specific, presented to Texas legislators, directly supports an RBP/direct-contracting pitch.
- **Social angle:** Bar chart per major TX health system — green bar = breakeven (% of Medicare), pink bar = RAND price paid (% of Medicare). Caption: "Texas hospitals only need X% of Medicare to break even. Employers are paying up to 163%."

### 2. Indiana caps hospital prices at 2.6x Medicare for employers, modeled on an Oregon cap that saved $107M and cut outpatient prices 25%
- **Source:** NPR, "States try new tactics to bring down high hospital prices," Aug 26, 2026 (reporting on Indiana's 2025 law/2026 compliance report and an independent two-year evaluation of Oregon's price-cap program). https://www.npr.org/2026/08/26/nx-s1-5939965/medical-price-hospital-healthcare-indiana
- **Methodology/sample:** State law analysis plus a 2026 Indiana state compliance report on hospital pricing behavior; Oregon figures from an independent two-year evaluation of its all-payer/public-employee price-cap program.
- **The number:** Indiana's law (effective Sept 1, 2026) requires 75 hospitals to offer employer deals at ≤2.6x Medicare rates. Oregon's comparable cap produced **>$107 million in savings and a 25% drop in outpatient price per procedure over two years** — with the caveat that some hospitals previously billing below the cap raised prices up to it.
- **Why it matters:** A live, real-world natural experiment in reference-based pricing at the state level — hard evidence RBP/direct-contracting caps can move prices, with a documented "cap becomes floor" risk that self-funded plan sponsors should build into contract design.
- **Texas APCD / direct-contracting flag:** Directly relevant — this is reference-based pricing policy in action.
- **Social angle:** "What happens when a state caps hospital prices at 2.6x Medicare? Oregon tried it — $107M saved, prices down 25%. Indiana just copied it." LinkedIn thread walking through the RBP mechanism and the cap-becomes-floor caution.

### 3. 88% of U.S. hospital markets are now "highly or very highly concentrated" — and price, not utilization, drives the 2x spending gap between metros
- **Source:** Health Care Cost Institute (HCCI), *Health Cost Landscape* — "Metro Health Care Costs Vary by More than Twofold Nationwide," April 9, 2026. https://healthcostinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/HCCI-HCL-release-FINAL.pdf (interactive tool: https://hcl.healthcostinstitute.org/)
- **Methodology/sample:** 1.3+ billion medical claims, 2018–2022, covering 38+ million people/year with employer-sponsored insurance, across 269 metro areas in 45 states.
- **The number:** 88% of metro hospital markets studied are highly/very highly concentrated. Health care spending varies by **more than 2x** between metros (Charleston, WV highest vs. Bakersfield, CA lowest), driven by price rather than utilization. More than half of metros studied have a "high cost burden" (per-person spend >7% of average income).
- **Why it matters:** The largest available commercial-claims dataset confirms local market power — not overutilization — is the primary cost lever employers can pull, which is the foundational argument for site-of-care steering, narrow/direct networks, and unbundled contracting over broad-network status quo.
- **Texas APCD / direct-contracting flag:** Strong — this is the underlying market-failure diagnosis that direct-contracting/RBP positioning is built to solve.
- **Social angle:** Ranked bar chart or map of metro spending with Charleston/Bakersfield as the "2x" bookends. Thread: "It's not that people in [City] use more care. They just pay more for the same care."

### 4. Texas's own all-payer claims database can't see the self-funded employer plans that dominate the state's commercial market
- **Source:** UTHealth Houston Center for Health Care Data (TX-APCD program materials); Texas Orthopaedic Association "APCD 101" explainer; legal basis is *Gobeille v. Liberty Mutual Insurance Co.*, 578 U.S. 312 (2016). https://sph.uth.edu/research/centers/center-for-health-care-data/texas-all-payor-claims-database/ ; https://toa.org/apcd-101-understanding-the-texas-all-payor-claims-database/
- **Methodology/sample:** Program documentation on TX-APCD data submission requirements and researcher access timeline (approved-researcher access begins March 2026).
- **The number:** TX-APCD will hold claims on ~60% of covered Texans — "nearly 100% of medical claims regulated by the state" — but under *Gobeille*, states cannot compel ERISA self-funded employer plans (or Medicare FFS) to submit data, so self-funded participation is voluntary only.
- **Why it matters:** The state's flagship price-transparency dataset structurally excludes exactly the population — self-funded ERISA plan sponsors — that a self-funded/direct-contracting consultancy serves, which is the clearest argument for why employers need private benchmarking data (RAND, NASHP, HCCI) rather than relying on the state APCD alone.
- **Texas APCD / direct-contracting flag:** **This is the TX APCD data-gap finding, flagged as required by the brief.**
- **Social angle:** "Texas just built a statewide claims database to fight rising costs. It legally can't see your self-funded plan's data. Here's why that matters if you're self-insured." Explainer thread format.

### 5. Mercer: 2026 employer health benefit costs rising 6.7% — the steepest jump since 2010 — with GLP-1s a named driver
- **Source:** Mercer, National Survey of Employer-Sponsored Health Plans (preliminary 2026 results), press release Nov 18, 2025. https://www.mercer.com/en-us/about/newsroom/employers-and-workers-face-affordability-crunch-as-health-insurnace-cost-is-expected-to-exceed-18500-per-employee-in-2026/
- **Methodology/sample:** Survey of 1,700+ US employers.
- **The number:** Total health benefit cost per employee projected to rise 6.7% in 2026 to **$18,500+** (from $17,496 in 2025) — the largest increase since 2010; without plan changes the increase would have been ~9%. Rx spend rose 9.4% among large employers; GLP-1 coverage among large employers (500+) rose to 49% (from 44% in 2024); 59% of employers plan cost-cutting changes for 2026 (up from 44% in 2024); 51% of large employers say they're likely to shift more cost to employees via deductible/OOP-max changes.
- **Why it matters:** Quantifies the affordability pressure pushing employers toward alternative funding/network strategies — the "why now" backdrop for a direct-contracting or unbundled-TPA pitch, showing status-quo plan design is running out of room.
- **Texas APCD / direct-contracting flag:** Indirect — establishes urgency rather than a direct RBP data point.
- **Social angle:** Trend line of YoY cost-increase % 2010–2026, spiking this year, annotated "highest since 2010" — pairs well with a "what employers are doing about it" follow-up post.
