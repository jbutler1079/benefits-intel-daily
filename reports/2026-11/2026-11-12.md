### Direct-contracted center-of-excellence episodes cost 137% of Medicare, compared with 320% at the insurer's negotiated rate: $19,090 (59%) less per episode across 370 self-funded plans
- **Source:** Murray RC, Whaley C., "Negotiated prices in a Medicare-benchmarked center-of-excellence network compared with insurer-negotiated commercial rates," *Health Affairs Scholar* 4(11), qxag278, published Oct 15, 2026 (open access; DOI 10.1093/haschl/qxag278; PMC13739050). Funded by Arnold Ventures and the Peterson Health Technology Institute.
- **Methodology/sample:** The data come from Lantern's COE program: about 370 self-funded employer plans, 3.8M people in 49 states, 33,448 services, and about $80B in medical spending from 2022 to 2025. COE providers accept episode rates set relative to Medicare as payment in full. Each COE episode is compared with the employer's own insurer-negotiated rate in the same market. The regression uses year, MSA and procedure fixed effects, with errors clustered by MSA. The authors call the differences *negotiated price-schedule* differences, not causal savings, because COE providers are partly selected on price. The study does not measure quality outcomes. I read the full paper on PMC.
- **The number:** After regression adjustment, prevailing commercial rates averaged **320% of Medicare**, compared with **137%** in the COE network, a gap of 183 points (57%). Per episode, the averages were **$32,182 vs. $13,092**, a gap of **$19,090 (59%)**, for about **$640M** less in total payments. Every service category had a gap of at least 167 points: GI was 337% vs. 170%, and pain management was 356% vs. 133%. The highest prevailing rate was ENT at 357% of Medicare, with a per-episode gap of $61,609. COE spine was the lowest rate at 113% of Medicare. All differences were significant at P < 0.01.
- **Why it matters:** This is the largest peer-reviewed dataset so far showing that self-funded employers can contract directly at about 1.4x Medicare for shoppable episodes, while their own carrier network pays more than 3x Medicare for the same services in the same markets. It pairs with June's RBP study (122% vs. 240% inpatient), which found a similar ~57% gap using a different mechanism.
- **Social angle:** A bar chart of six service lines, showing "% of Medicare: your carrier vs. direct contract." LinkedIn hook: "Same city. Same procedure. Your carrier paid 320% of Medicare. The direct contract paid 137%." Add a carousel slide on the authors' caveat (no quality data, not a causal estimate) to keep credibility.
- **Flags:** **Direct-contracting positioning: strong tie.** This is close to the textbook case for unbundled TPA plus COE carve-out. **TX-APCD: indirect.** The data come from a vendor's claims, not public data, which shows that employers need their own claims and rate data to run this comparison.

### Transparency files show 3.0x price spread for the same joint replacement within Dallas–Fort Worth, and an Optum-owned Houston clinic paid up to ~70% above market for office visits
- **Source:** Trilliant Health, "Price Transparency in Healthcare: Examining the Value Behind Hospital and Health Plan Transparency," Nov 10, 2026 (Petrucelli, Patton, Oakes).
- **Methodology/sample:** The report uses hospital machine-readable files and payer Transparency in Coverage files for UHC and Aetna. It compares hospital-posted and payer-posted rates for the same payer-procedure pairs, and it measures rate spreads by MS-DRG/CPT, CBSA and hospital. It also tracks mastectomy (CPT 19307) rates in 47 CBSAs from 2024 to 2026 and correlates rates with mortality. Neither data source includes volume, and sampling and cleaning steps are not fully documented. This is a vendor report, not peer-reviewed. I read it on Trilliant's site.
- **The number:**
  - **DFW MS-DRG 470** (major joint replacement): median **$24,134**, the lowest of the CBSAs shown. Within the market, rates ranged from **$21,604 to $64,637 (3.0x)**. Nationally, Aetna DRG 470 rates ranged **8.8x** ($13,290–$117,081).
  - **Houston:** UHC's E/M rates at Kelsey-Seybold, which is owned by UnitedHealth/Optum, ran from **~5% (99211) to nearly 70% (99215) above market average.**
  - **Site of care:** for UHC CPT 29827 (rotator cuff repair), the HOPD median was **$13,477** and the ASC median was **$5,492**.
  - **Hospital vs. payer files:** only **10.8%** of rate pairs matched exactly, and **17.9%** were off by more than 1.5x in either direction.
  - **Mastectomy rates, 2024–26:** rose faster than CPI-U (6.6%) in **26 of 47** markets.
  - **Hospital MRF compliance:** **49.4%** as of Aug 2026.
- **Why it matters:** Texas employers can see a 3x spread for the same DRG inside one metro, and an insurer appears to pay its own affiliated clinic more. Both show why carrier networks don't price-discipline themselves, and why steerage or direct contracts within a single market can capture savings. The hospital-vs-payer mismatch is also a warning: a plan should not rely on one public file to set a reference price.
- **Social angle:** Houston vertical-integration chart (Kelsey-Seybold E/M premium by visit level) as a short LinkedIn post: "When the insurer owns the clinic, who negotiates for you?" Second post: DFW joint-replacement range ($21.6K–$64.6K) as a single stat card.
- **Flags:** **Direct-contracting positioning: strong tie,** since it covers in-market variation and HOPD vs. ASC. **TX-APCD gap: yes.** Public MRF/TiC files are the only public source of commercial negotiated rates in Texas markets, but they have no volume data and the two sources often disagree. TX-APCD doesn't include large self-funded plans. Employers still need their own claims to weight these rates.
