### 95% of large employers that got their claims data say it helped, yet 1 in 4 never asked. Facing a 2026 cost spike, 44% shifted premiums onto workers and only 7% touched their network
- **Source:**
  - **Primary:** KFF, *2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey*, Section 12, "Employer Access to Information About the Cost and Use of Medical Services by Plan Enrollees," Figures 12.21, 12.24 and 12.25, plus Summary of Findings Figure D. Released Oct. 2026.
  - **Secondary:** Trilliant Health (Patton & Oakes), *Employer Access to Health Plan Data Has Not Yet Translated Into Value-Based Benefit Design*, Nov. 19, 2026.
- **Methodology/sample:**
  - The survey covers **1,937** randomly selected non-federal public and private firms with 10+ workers, fielded in 2026.
  - The data-access questions were asked only of **firms with 200+ workers that offer benefits**, about their largest-enrollment plan.
  - All figures below were verified against the KFF full-report PDF. These questions are **new in 2026**, so there is no trend line yet.
- **The number:**
  - **Getting claims data:** 27% of large firms say it is "very easy" and 25% "somewhat easy." **17%** say it is somewhat or very difficult, **25% have never tried**, and 5% don't know.
  - **Value once obtained:** **66%** say claims data helped "a great deal" with benefit decisions, and 29% say "somewhat." That is **95% helpful** and only **1% "not at all."**
  - **Who isn't asking:** per Trilliant's cut of the KFF data, **28%** of firms with 200–999 workers have never tried, against **9%** of firms with 5,000+ workers.
  - **Price visibility:** only **14%** of large firms "strongly agree" they can see what their plan pays in-network providers and how those prices compare. **23%** disagree and **17%** don't know.
  - **What large firms did about projected 2026 cost increases:**
    - Raised the worker premium share: **44%**
    - Raised deductibles or other cost sharing: **21%**
    - Changed insurer or administrator: **16%**
    - Added prior authorization or other utilization management: **10%**
    - Moved to a tiered or narrower network: **7%**
  - **Context:** **89%** of all employers describe their largest plan's network as "very" or "somewhat" broad.
  - **Reconciliation:** Trilliant's conclusion also cites 18% and 5%. Those are the **all-firm** figures (18% raised the worker premium share, 5% narrowed the network), not the large-firm figures above.
- **Why it matters:**
  - This is the cleanest national evidence yet that **data access is not the bottleneck; using it is**. Almost every employer that pulls its claims finds them useful. Even so, the main response to cost growth is still to send the bill to employees, not to change where care is bought.
  - For mid-size self-funded employers (200–999 lives), the gap is widest: more than **1 in 4 have never asked their TPA or carrier for claims data**, and only 1 in 7 large employers is confident it can see provider prices.
  - Under ERISA and the CAA gag-clause ban, a self-funded plan sponsor is entitled to these data. Not asking is a fiduciary exposure as well as a missed opportunity.
  - **Ties directly to unbundled/direct-contracting positioning.** An unbundled TPA and direct-contract model is built on the plan owning its claims and price data, then using them to steer care, which is the "7%" lever most employers aren't pulling.
  - **TX-APCD gap:** TX-APCD excludes self-funded ERISA plan claims. A Texas employer therefore cannot benchmark its own prices against a public all-payer source; its own claims feed is the only local price data it will get. **Flag as a TX-APCD data gap.**
- **Social angle:**
  - **Chart:** a horizontal bar of the large-firm cost-response options, with 44% "made workers pay more" towering over 7% "changed the network." Caption: "Employers had the data. They used the wallet instead."
  - **LinkedIn take:** "95% of employers who pulled their claims data said it helped. 25% have never asked. If you're self-funded, that data is yours by law. Ask for it before your next renewal."
  - **Thread hook:** "Only 14% of large employers strongly agree they know what their plan pays providers. You can't negotiate a price you can't see."
  - **Pairing:** run it with the Nov. 13 item (11.9 vs. 7.9 cost strategies used by employers with full vs. limited data access). That item shows data access correlates with action; this one shows most employers haven't taken the first step.

### Watch items (not findings)
- **INET Working Paper No. 256 (Introcaso & Cunningham, Oct. 16, 2026):**
  - **Argument:** that five years of price-transparency mandates have produced price *convergence*, not cost reduction, because anti-steering, anti-tiering and all-or-nothing clauses remove buyers' outside option.
  - **Proposal:** an independently published, non-binding procedure-level reference price, plus a ban on those contract clauses.
  - **Figures cited:** CBO's earlier ~$4.9B 10-year deficit-reduction score for a ban on those clauses, and Oregon's state-plan cap saving **$107.5M in 27 months**.
  - **Caveat:** this is a policy proposal, not new data. The authors founded Sylk Health, which publishes committed procedure prices from accredited hospitals and clinics outside the US.
  - **Possible use:** a framing post if paired with primary data.
- **RAND Hospital Price Transparency Round 6:** today (Nov. 20, 2026) is the scheduled data-delivery completion date. The public report is expected **Sept. 2027**. If Texas self-funded clients or HPHP contributed data, now is the time to confirm.
- **FDA, Nov. 16, 2026:** pertuzumab-qcay (Pebrilzo), a Perjeta biosimilar for HER2+ breast cancer, was approved. It is another oncology-infusion biosimilar and relevant to specialty site-of-care and biosimilar-first policies. No pricing yet.
