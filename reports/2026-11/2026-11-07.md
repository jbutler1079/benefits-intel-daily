### Provider-sponsored health plans negotiated lower prices than nonintegrated insurers for 44 of 46 shared services
- **Source:** Health Affairs, "Provider-Sponsored Health Plans Negotiated Lower Prices For Health Services Compared With Traditional Insurers, 2024," vol. 45, no. 10 (Oct 2026), DOI 10.1377/hlthaff.2025.01531
- **Methodology/sample:** Hospital price transparency data (period reported as 2020–23 by a Health Affairs roundup) comparing negotiated prices for the 46 mandatory-reported shoppable services; about 28% of health systems in the study operated their own plan as of 2023. Full text was paywalled (403), so details come from the abstract and TechTarget coverage.
- **The number:** Prices were significantly lower for provider-sponsored plans on 44 of 46 services. Largest reported gap: Level 1 ED facility fee, $216 vs. $277 (22% lower).
- **Why it matters:** Price differences within the same hospitals depend on who is the payer and how the contract is structured, not on what the service costs to deliver. That supports an employer argument for direct contracting and unbundled arrangements instead of accepting a carrier or TPA network rate by default. Caveat: provider-owned plans have a built-in incentive to steer members to their own system, so the comparison is not apples-to-apples for an employer.
- **Social angle:** LinkedIn take or two-bar chart: "Same hospital, same ED visit: $216 vs. $277. The difference is the payer, not the care." Verify the full text before posting any figure beyond the two above.
- **Flags:** Ties to the unbundled/direct-contracting positioning. TX-APCD angle: none identified.
