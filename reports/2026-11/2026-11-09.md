### DOL finalizes PBM fee-disclosure rule for large self-insured plans: rebates, spread and pharmacy clawbacks must now be disclosed; DOL projects up to $22B in savings over 10 years
- **Source:** U.S. DOL / EBSA, final rule "Improving Transparency Into Pharmacy Benefit Manager Fee Disclosure," News Release 26-1557-NAT, Oct 29, 2026 (Fed. Reg. doc 2026-22306). Implements Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (PBMs as covered service providers under ERISA 408(b)(2)(B)).
- **Methodology/sample:** Regulation, not a study. The $22B figure is DOL's own regulatory-impact projection (undiscounted, 10 years). I read the DOL news release; the 350-page rule text was not read. Details beyond the release (semiannual/quarterly updates, 5% variance explanation, annual audit rights with 10-business-day acknowledgment and 60-day delivery, $1,000 broker/consultant threshold) come from trade press (Managed Healthcare Executive) and are unverified against the rule text.
- **The number:** Plans covering ~90 million Americans; up to $22 billion projected savings over 10 years. For the first time PBMs must disclose (1) manufacturer rebates/payments, (2) spread compensation where plan price exceeds pharmacy reimbursement, and (3) pharmacy clawbacks.
- **Why it matters:** Disclosure creates a fiduciary data trail: plan sponsors who ignore it have a harder time defending PBM arrangements. Effective/applicability date and the definition of "large" plan were not stated in the sources I read, so confirm before posting a compliance deadline.
- **Social angle:** LinkedIn take: "DOL just made PBM spread, rebates and clawbacks a disclosure item. If you're self-funded, the question is no longer 'can I see it?' but 'what is my fiduciary process for acting on it?'" Avoid stating dates or plan-size cutoffs until verified.
- **Flags:** Ties to unbundled/direct-contracting positioning (transparent, pass-through compensation; audit rights). TX-APCD angle: none.

### Employers with full claim-level data access use 11.9 of 26 cost strategies vs 7.9 for those without
- **Source:** National Alliance of Healthcare Purchaser Coalitions, 2026 Pulse of the Purchaser (released Aug 11, 2026), as reported by AJMC (Aug 12, 2026).
- **Methodology/sample:** Online survey of 408 employers, fielded May–June 2026; 24.4% had 10,000+ employees, 37.5% 1,000–9,999, 38.1% under 1,000. Sampling method, weighting and margin of error not reported in the article. I read the AJMC summary, not the report.
- **The number:** About one-third of employers lack complete claim-level access; only about 3 in 5 feel confident they could audit full claims files. Full-access employers used an average 11.9 of 26 strategies vs 7.9 for limited/no/uncertain access. Hospital/facility = 30.5% of spend, Rx 21.1%, professional 19.1%. Big 3 PBM use fell from 63.4% (2025) to 54.3% (2026). 84.6% support hospital price transparency; 82.6% support hospital rate regulation.
- **Why it matters:** Data access correlates with action, though this is correlation from a survey, not proof of causation. Hospital/facility is the largest cost bucket, and it is the one least addressed by PBM reform.
- **Social angle:** Single-stat graphic: "11.9 vs 7.9. Employers with full claims access pull ~50% more cost levers." Caveat in caption: survey, self-reported.
- **Flags:** Ties directly to unbundled/direct-contracting positioning (claims data ownership). Possible TX-APCD tie: where employers lack plan data, APCD gaps matter more, but the survey says nothing on APCDs. Not a finding about TX.
