# Daily Research — 2026-11-17

**Three findings met the bar today. None has appeared in this project's coverage before.**
1. **EBRI Issue Brief No. 669 (Nov. 12, 2026):** No Surprises Act arbitration (federal IDR) awards added **$8.39B** in plan payments above what plans first paid, for disputes closed in 2025. That is about **$46–50 per covered person per year**, and providers won **85–88%** of determinations.
2. **Lake, Casalino et al., *Health Affairs* (Feb. 2026):** after Optum bought 24 surgery centers, prices to *competing* commercial insurers rose **11% (+$239 per procedure)**. Market share did not rise; the authors put the increase down to vertical integration.
3. **CMS CY 2027 OPPS/ASC final rule (Nov. 4, 2026):** using its new 340B acquisition-cost survey, CMS cut Medicare payment for 340B drugs to **ASP − 28%**. It also found cases where the patient's 20% coinsurance was *more than the hospital paid for the drug*. Commercial plans get none of this.

The sweep found no new RAND, NASHP, HCCI, PBGH, FTC, GAO (PBM/pricing), CBO, BLS or TX-APCD release in the past 7 days. WTW's Oct. 28 survey was checked: its headline numbers (11.1% for 2027, PBM switching) were already covered in earlier reports.

### No Surprises Act arbitration added $8.39B to employer plan costs in 2025, about $46–50 per covered person. Providers won 85–88% of disputes, and CMS logged 2.56M disputes against a projected 22,000 a year
- **Source:** Employee Benefit Research Institute (Paul Fronstin), *The Federal Independent Dispute Resolution Process and Employment-Based Health Plan Costs*, EBRI Issue Brief No. 669, Nov. 12, 2026. The study was funded by Pivotal Health Technologies, Inc.
- **Methodology/sample:**
  - **Data:** CMS's 2025 federal IDR "QPA and Offers" public-use files, four quarters, **5.78M line items** (4.41M usable for the QPA comparison).
  - **What was measured:** the "additional plan payment obligation," meaning the IDR award minus the plan's initial payment minus patient cost sharing.
  - **How the full year was built:**
    - Q3–Q4 were measured directly from **2.47M line items**, using the initial-payment fields CMS began publishing in Q3 2025.
    - Q1–Q2 were estimated from the award-minus-QPA difference, using the ratio observed in the second half (1.0057).
    - Lines with differences above ±$1M (27 lines) were excluded as likely data errors.
  - **Denominator:** **$1.342T** in national employment-based plan costs, built from AHRQ MEPS-IC (private sector, including **$595.2B** self-insured premium-equivalents), state and local government, and FEHB.
- **The number:**
  - **Total:** **$8.39B** in added plan obligations for 2025 (**$3.66B** in H1 and **$4.73B** in H2).
  - **Share of cost:** **0.625%** of all employment-based plan costs, or **$46–50 per covered person per year**.
  - **Sensitivity:**
    - With a $100K outlier cutoff, the total is **$8.02B**.
    - With no cutoff, it is **$10.57B**.
  - **Per dispute line (H2):**
    - Median added obligation: **$385–$391**.
    - Mean: **$1,845–$1,994**, a long right tail.
  - **Volume:** about **2.56M disputes** were initiated in 2025, against federal agencies' original projection of **~22,000 a year**.
  - **Outcomes:**
    - Providers, facilities and air ambulances prevailed in **~88%** of determinations in H1 2025 and **~85%** in H2.
    - In H2 2025, the winning offer exceeded the QPA in **~87%** of determinations.
  - **What it leaves out:** the out-of-network claims settled before IDR (industry estimates cited: 76% of initial payments accepted, 18% settled in negotiation, 6% reach IDR), IDR administrative costs, and any upward pressure on in-network rates.
- **Why it matters:** The No Surprises Act applies to self-funded ERISA plans, so this cost lands directly on employer claims. It concentrates in the specialties employers can't steer: emergency, anesthesia, radiology, pathology, neonatology and air ambulance. Every self-funded plan should track its IDR exposure, and **the hedge is contracting**. Direct agreements with the ER, anesthesia and radiology groups at the facilities members actually use remove those claims from IDR. Plans built on reference-based pricing should model this risk explicitly, because their out-of-network-style payments are the claims most likely to be disputed. **Ties to unbundled/direct-contracting positioning.**
  - **TX angle:** Texas groups have been among the heaviest IDR filers, and TX-APCD cannot show what self-funded plans pay out after IDR. **Flag as a TX-APCD data gap.**
  - **Caveat on the TX angle:** the Texas-heavy filing point comes from earlier CMS PUF reporting, not from this brief, which gives no state breakdown.
- **Social angle:**
  - **Chart:** "22,000 projected vs 2.56M actual" disputes, a bar chart with a log-scale callout.
  - **LinkedIn take:** "Providers win ~87% of surprise-billing arbitrations, and your self-funded plan pays the difference: ~$50 per member, before admin fees. Here's how direct contracts with hospital-based physicians take you out of the lottery."
  - **Note on funding:** say that EBRI's study was industry-funded, and that it is a conservative floor (IDR-only).

### When Optum bought surgery centers, prices to rival insurers rose 11% ($239 per procedure). Its market share didn't grow; the authors attribute the rise to vertical integration
- **Source:** Derek T. Lake, Lawrence P. Casalino et al. (Cornell), "Strategic Selection And Pricing Power: Optum's Acquisitions Of Ambulatory Surgery Centers And Physician Practices," *Health Affairs* 45(2):218–225, published Feb. 2, 2026.
- **Methodology/sample:**
  - **Prices:** difference-in-differences on commercial claims, 2015–2018, for **24 ASCs** Optum acquired through its 2017 purchase of Surgical Care Affiliates, against matched controls. UnitedHealthcare claims were excluded, so the price effect is on *competing* insurers.
  - **Referrals:** Medicare FFS claims, 2013–2021, for **21 acquired physician practices**, with controls.
- **The number:**
  - **Price per procedure:** up **11%**, or an average of **+$239**, relative to controls. The increase appeared within about two quarters and persisted.
  - **Professional fees** rose only for procedures by Optum-employed physicians. **Facility fees** rose whether the surgeon was employed or independent.
  - **Where the increases landed:** in markets with high horizontal ASC share *or* heavy vertical integration with co-located practices.
  - **Market share:** Optum did **not** increase horizontal ASC concentration.
  - **Added spending:**
    - Seven common procedures added **~$10.1M a year**.
    - Those procedures are about 15% of ASC volume, so the authors extrapolate to **>$67M a year** across all ASC services.
  - **Referrals:**
    - Acquired practices **already** sent **5.1 percentage points** more patients to ASCs than expected before purchase.
    - There was **no significant shift in site of care** after acquisition.
  - **Limitations:** small sample and short post-acquisition window.
- **Why it matters:** "Move it to an ASC" is a standard site-of-care savings lever, but this study shows the ASC price itself is negotiable leverage, and it rises when the owner is also an insurer and physician employer. Employers can't assume an ASC is cheap. They need ASC-level price data, ideally from their own claims or transparency files, and contracts priced per bundled case that cover both facility and professional fees. Merger metrics that look only at horizontal concentration miss this effect, so antitrust agencies won't flag it for you. **Ties directly to unbundled/direct-contracting positioning:** independent-ASC direct bundles are the counter.
  - **TX angle:** Texas has dense, often physician-owned ASC markets, and USPI/Tenet and SCA/Optum are both large there. TX-APCD's self-funded gap means the state can't measure this effect locally. **Flag as a TX-APCD data gap.**
- **Social angle:**
  - **Thread:** "Same surgery center, new owner, +11% price, and patients went to the same places. Who owns your ASC matters."
  - **Chart:** before/after price line from the study's event-study figure.
  - **LinkedIn take:** independent ASC direct bundles vs. insurer-owned ASC network rates.

### Medicare now pays 340B hospitals ASP − 28% for drugs after a CMS survey found some patients' 20% coinsurance exceeded what the hospital paid. Commercial plans still pay full rates on those drugs
- **Source:** CMS, Calendar Year 2027 Hospital Outpatient Prospective Payment System (OPPS) and ASC Final Rule (CMS-1850-F), and the CMS fact sheet, issued Nov. 4, 2026.
- **Methodology/sample:**
  - **340B acquisition-cost survey:** CMS surveyed 340B hospitals' drug acquisition costs from Jan. 1 to Apr. 7, 2026.
  - **Scope:** the final rule applies to about 3,500 hospitals and 6,400 ASCs.
- **The number:**
  - **340B drugs:** paid at **ASP − 28%** in 2027.
    - CMS found 340B acquisition costs "differed significantly" from non-340B costs.
    - In some cases the beneficiary's **20% coinsurance exceeded the hospital's purchase price** for the drug.
  - **Year-one effect of the 340B cut:**
    - **−$4.35B** in Medicare drug payments.
    - **−$1.1B** in beneficiary drug payments, about **$900** saved per affected beneficiary.
    - It is budget-neutral: non-drug OPPS payments rise by an equal amount.
  - **Remedy offset:** the 340B remedy offset on non-drug payments rises from **0.5% to 2%** until $7.8B is recouped, which CMS estimates happens in **2031**.
  - **Site-neutral imaging:** off-campus HOPD imaging without contrast (x-ray, ultrasound, CT, MRI, DXA) is paid at the Physician Fee Schedule-equivalent rate. Savings are about **$260M** in year one: **$190M** Part B plus **$70M** beneficiary.
  - **Inpatient-only list:** **637 services** come off it in year 2 of a 3-year phase-out.
  - **Rate update:** OPPS/ASC update of **+2.3%**.
- **Why it matters:** Medicare now has survey-based evidence of how far below ASP 340B hospitals buy drugs. Commercial plans, including self-funded ones, typically pay those same hospitals a percent-of-charges or ASP-plus rate on the same infusions. Employers have a citable government benchmark here for:
  - **Carving specialty infusions out** to home or non-340B sites.
  - **Negotiating ASP-based caps** in direct hospital contracts.
  - **Asking TPAs** what share of their specialty-drug spend runs through 340B hospital outpatient departments.

  Medicare's site-neutral imaging move does the same for imaging: Medicare will no longer pay hospital rates for an off-campus x-ray or MRI, so employers shouldn't either. **Ties to unbundled/direct-contracting positioning** (site-of-care steering, specialty carve-outs).
  - **Caveat:** these are Medicare FFS rules, and litigation over 340B payment cuts is likely. Earlier 340B cuts were struck down in *AHA v. Becerra* (2022), though this round rests on a new acquisition-cost survey, the gap the Court identified.
- **Social angle:**
  - **Single-stat graphic:** "Medicare found cases where a patient's 20% coinsurance was more than the hospital paid for the drug. What is your plan paying?"
  - **Short thread:** 340B economics for self-funded employers.
  - **Site-neutral carousel:** the same MRI on-campus, off-campus and at a freestanding center.
