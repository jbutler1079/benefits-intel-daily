# Daily Research — 2026-11-18

**Three findings met the bar today. None has appeared in this project's coverage before.**
1. **Leapfrog Fall 2026 Hospital Safety Grades + PBGH Health Care Data Demonstration (released today, Nov. 18, 2026):** In Seattle, an "A"-graded hospital negotiated **195–239% of Medicare** while a "C"-graded hospital in the same market negotiated **350–467%**. PBGH found little to no correlation between price and quality across 5 self-insured purchasers in 10 markets. Separately, about **450 non-participating hospitals now get no grade** after a federal court ruling won by Tenet-owned hospitals.
2. **Day, Cutler & Guerriere, *Health Affairs* (Oct. 2026):** BCBS Massachusetts commercial spending per member rose **11.8%** in FY2025, but inflation-adjusted **price growth was only 1.3%**. Utilization (+4.8%), service mix, and rising **coding intensity** drove the rise: the share of ED visits billed at the two highest levels went from 52% to 58%. This cuts against the "it's all prices" narrative.
3. **Marsh (Mercer) 2026 National Survey of Employer-Sponsored Health Plans, initial results (Nov. 17, 2026):** large-employer GLP-1 weight-loss coverage **fell from 49% to 41%** in one year (66% → 58% at 20,000+ employers), even as Rx cost growth hit **9.7%**. The survey's 8.2% 2027 projection was already reported, so only the new GLP-1 and network figures are used here.

### An "A" hospital in Seattle negotiated 195–239% of Medicare; a "C" hospital in the same market negotiated 350–467%. Meanwhile ~450 hospitals lost their Leapfrog grade after a court loss
- **Source:**
  - The Leapfrog Group, *Fall 2026 Hospital Safety Grades* press release, Nov. 18, 2026.
  - Purchaser Business Group on Health (PBGH), *Health Care Data Demonstration Initiative*, as cited in the Leapfrog release.
  - Grade-count and court-ruling details also from *Chief Healthcare Executive*, Nov. 18, 2026.
- **Methodology/sample:**
  - **Leapfrog grades:** ~2,400 general acute-care hospitals graded on errors, accidents, injuries and infections, using CMS data plus the Leapfrog Hospital Survey.
    - **Change this cycle:** Staff Responsiveness (H-COMP-3) was temporarily removed, with a temporary scoring adjustment.
  - **PBGH demonstration:**
    - Combined employer claims, federal Transparency in Coverage negotiated-rate files, and quality/safety measures, including Leapfrog grades.
    - Covered **5 large self-insured purchasers** across **10 U.S. markets**.
    - Prices were expressed as a percent of a national Medicare benchmark.
- **The number:**
  - **Price vs. quality (PBGH):**
    - "Little to no correlation" between negotiated prices and quality measures, and some of the highest-priced hospitals had lower safety grades.
    - **Seattle:** the "A" hospital was at **195–239%** of Medicare; a "C" hospital in the same market was at **350–467%**. The lower-quality hospital was paid roughly **1.8–2.0x** as much.
  - **Fall 2026 grade distribution (2,367 graded):**
    - **900 A (38.0%)**, **774 B (32.7%)**, **655 C (27.7%)**, **35 D**, **3 F**.
    - Connecticut leads with **64.3%** A. Iowa, North Dakota, South Dakota, Vermont and Wyoming have **no A hospitals**.
  - **Lost grades:**
    - About **450 hospitals** that did not complete the Leapfrog survey are now "Grade Not Assigned."
    - The cause: a March 2026 federal ruling in South Florida that sided with **five Tenet-owned hospitals**.
    - Leapfrog extended the change to all non-participants and is appealing. Leah Binder: "Four hundred fifty communities in the United States have no access to a hospital safety grade."
- **Why it matters:** This is the clearest primary-source data point yet for "price and quality are unrelated." It undercuts the most common hospital defense of high commercial rates. For a self-funded employer it argues for steering and direct contracts built on **both** price (TiC files) and safety grades, and for refusing to pay a premium that doesn't buy quality. The ungraded-hospital change matters too: if a system declines to report, employers lose a free quality signal and should ask for it as a contract term. **Ties directly to unbundled/direct-contracting positioning:** pairing quality with price is how a network gets built.
  - **TX angle:** Texas ranked **12th** in spring 2026 with **38.1%** A hospitals (DFW Business Group on Health / D Magazine). Its fall 2026 rank is in Leapfrog's state PDF and was not confirmed today.
    - Tenet operates a large Texas footprint, so some Texas facilities may now be ungraded. **Unconfirmed: check HospitalSafetyGrade.org before posting.**
    - TX-APCD does not cover self-funded plans, so no public Texas dataset links what employers actually pay to these grades. PBGH's method (TiC files plus grades) is a workable substitute. **Flag as a TX-APCD data gap.**
- **Social angle:**
  - **Chart:** dumbbell chart, "A hospital: 195–239% of Medicare vs. C hospital: 350–467%, same city."
  - **LinkedIn take:** "There's no good price for bad care. Your carrier network pays the C hospital nearly double. A direct contract doesn't have to."
  - **Follow-up post:** a DFW/Houston/Amarillo version once the Texas fall grades are pulled from HospitalSafetyGrade.org.

### BCBS Massachusetts commercial spending rose 11.8% in a year, but inflation-adjusted prices rose only 1.3%. Utilization, service mix and coding intensity did the rest, and the share of ED visits at the top two billing levels rose from 52% to 58%
- **Source:** Matthew P. Day (BCBSMA), David M. Cutler (Harvard/NBER) and Michael Guerriere (BCBSMA Chief Actuary), "Spending Growth In A Large Massachusetts Insurer Driven By Volume And Intensity," *Health Affairs* 45(10), published online Oct. 5, 2026. Coverage from *Boston Globe* (Oct. 7), *TechTarget* (Oct. 8) and *Becker's*. The paper itself is paywalled, and the figures below come from that coverage.
- **Methodology/sample:**
  - BCBSMA claims and administrative data, 2018 through H1 2025.
  - Covers fully insured, **self-insured** and Marketplace members: about 2M medical and 1.4M pharmacy enrollees, ~40% of Massachusetts commercial membership.
  - Spending growth was decomposed into utilization, inflation-adjusted price and service mix.
- **The number:**
  - **Spending growth per member:** **1.2%** (2019), then **6.8%** (FY2024), then **11.8%** (FY ending June 2025).
  - **Decomposition (per Becker's):**
    - Of **9.2 points** of inflation-adjusted growth, utilization contributed **4.1 points**, price **1.1**, and service mix **0.5**.
    - Utilization grew **4.8%**; real prices grew **1.3%**.
    - The remaining ~3.5 points are not broken out in the coverage, so check the paper before citing a full split.
  - **Drugs:**
    - Real retail Rx growth was **~19%** in FY2025, against **1.7%** pre-pandemic.
    - GLP-1 spending rose **83.7%**, compared with **9.6%** for all other retail drugs.
    - Covered-drug PMPM went from **$32 → $54** (2019 → FY2025, +68%).
  - **Coding intensity:**
    - Septicemia rose from **1.7% → 2.5%** of inpatient medical admissions (2019 → FY2025).
    - ED visits at the two highest complexity levels rose from **52% → 58%**. Office-visit intensity also rose.
    - The authors did not size how much of the growth coding explains. They point to AI coding tools, ambient scribes and EMR modules as possible drivers.
  - **The authors' conclusion:** curbing "fee schedule growth" alone is "unlikely to be sufficient."
- **Why it matters:** Most self-funded messaging (including this project's) leans on "prices, not utilization" (HCCI 2019–2023). This newer, larger-insurer data shows that the **post-2022 acceleration is volume, intensity and drugs**, and a better unit price won't stop that. A rate deal won't contain upcoding. Self-funded plans need claims-level visibility to audit ED/E&M level creep and sepsis DRGs. A carrier ASO that is itself paid on volume has little reason to provide it. **Ties to unbundled positioning:** an independent TPA plus a payment-integrity vendor, with the employer owning the data, can see and contest coding drift. A bundled carrier often won't. Use the study as a complement to the price story, not a contradiction: price explains the *level* of commercial costs, while this explains the *recent growth*.
  - **TX angle:** Texas has no equivalent public view of coding-level trend for self-funded lives, because TX-APCD excludes most ERISA plans. **Flag as a TX-APCD data gap.**
- **Social angle:**
  - **Chart:** stacked bar decomposing 9.2 points: utilization 4.1, price 1.1, mix 0.5.
  - **Thread:** "Your ER bill didn't get pricier. It got 'sicker.' 52% → 58% of ED visits now billed at the top two levels."
  - **LinkedIn take:** "Negotiating rates is half the job; auditing what gets billed is the other half."

### Large employers are pulling back on GLP-1 weight-loss coverage (49% to 41% in one year) even as pharmacy cost growth hits 9.7%
- **Source:** Marsh (Mercer), *2026 National Survey of Employer-Sponsored Health Plans*, initial results press release, Nov. 17, 2026. The full report is due March 2027.
- **Methodology/sample:** **2,000 employers**, fielded June–August 2026. "Large" means 500+ employees; "very large" means 20,000+.
- **The number:**
  - **GLP-1 weight-loss coverage:**
    - Large employers: **49% (2025) → 41% (2026)**.
    - Very large employers: **66% → 58%**.
    - Large employers requiring a targeted program for eligibility: **15% → 24%**.
    - GLP-1 list price cited: **~$1,000/month** before rebates.
  - **Pharmacy:** large-employer Rx cost growth was **9.7%** in 2026.
  - **Total cost:** **$18,777** per employee (+7.3%). *The 8.2% projection for 2027 was already reported, so it is not new here.*
  - **Employee contributions (PPO, monthly):**
    - Family: **$709 → $753**, about **+$528/yr**.
    - Single: **$191 → $202**.
  - **Narrow networks:** **31%** of large employers offer at least one plan steering to a smaller, higher-performing network.
- **Why it matters:** This is the first large-sample evidence that GLP-1 coverage peaked and is now being rationed, not just tightened. For a self-funded employer the choice is not "cover or don't." It is how to buy the drug: net price transparency, pass-through rebates, program-gated eligibility, and access to lower-cost options as they come. A spread-pricing PBM profits from the $1,000 list price either way. **Ties to unbundled/transparent-PBM positioning.**
  - **Context:** the 31% narrow-network figure pairs with the Leapfrog/PBGH finding above. Employers are already moving toward steering, but mostly on carrier-defined networks.
- **Social angle:**
  - **Chart:** slope chart, 66% → 58% and 49% → 41%.
  - **LinkedIn take:** "Employers aren't dropping GLP-1s because they don't work. They're dropping them because they can't see the net price. Fix the PBM contract before you cut the benefit."
