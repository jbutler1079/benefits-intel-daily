### Medical-care CPI inflation (1.7% y/y) is now running well below headline inflation (3.4% y/y) — a reversal of the usual pattern
- **Source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Consumer Price Index Summary, July 2026 data, released August 12, 2026. Direct WebFetch to bls.gov was blocked; figures cross-confirmed across two independent WebSearch passes pulling from the BLS release text, [BLS CPI Summary](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm), and corroborating coverage including [NBC News' July 2026 CPI report](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/economy/cpi-inflation-july-2026-rcna591698).
- **Methodology/sample:** BLS Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U), U.S. city average, not seasonally adjusted 12-month change, July 2026 release (covering data through July 2026).
- **The number:** The medical care index rose **1.7% year-over-year** in July 2026 — well below the **3.4%** all-items headline CPI. Underneath that: medical care **services** rose **2.9%** y/y, while medical care **commodities** (largely prescription drugs) **fell 2.1%** y/y, the divergence dragging the blended medical-care figure below the general inflation rate.
- **Why it matters:** This flips the usual "healthcare costs outpace inflation" framing that benefits conversations default to — headline CPI is currently running hotter than medical care overall, driven by falling drug-commodity prices offsetting still-elevated services inflation (services trend, at 2.9%, is the number that maps to what self-funded plans actually feel in hospital/physician claims trend, and it's still outpacing wage growth in most sectors). Useful corrective for employers who assume every year is a "medical trend crisis" — the real story is a widening gap between services and commodities, not a uniform spike.
- **Social angle:** Two-line stat contrast: "Medical care CPI: 1.7% y/y. Headline CPI: 3.4% y/y. For once, healthcare isn't the inflation story — but look under the hood: services are still up 2.9%, drugs are down 2.1%." Good chart candidate (three-bar comparison: headline / medical services / medical commodities) for a LinkedIn post framed around "don't let the CPI headline fool you."
