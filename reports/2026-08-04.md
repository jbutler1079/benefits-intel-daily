# Daily Benefits Intelligence — 2026-08-04

### Indiana just proved a state-mandated direct-to-employer price cap actually works — hospitals came in up to 89 points under the ceiling
- **Source:** Indiana Department of Health, House Enrolled Act (HEA) 1004 compliance report, released ~April 23, 2026, covering 2025 performance
- **Methodology/sample:** State audit of direct-to-employer (DTE) contract pricing at Indiana's five required nonprofit hospital systems (Ascension St. Vincent, Community Health Network, Franciscan Health, Indiana University Health, Parkview Health Network) against the law's cap of 260% of full Medicare (inpatient + outpatient facility prices combined)
- **The number:** All five systems cleared the 260%-of-Medicare cap for 2025. The best performer, Ascension St. Vincent, came in at 171% of Medicare — 89 points under the ceiling. Independent (non-system) hospitals join the mandate Sept. 1, 2026; the law carries a $10,000-per-day noncompliance penalty, and by 2029 systems must bring aggregate prices to the statewide average or forfeit nonprofit status for a year.
- **Why it matters:** This is the first live, audited proof that a legislated DTE price ceiling produces real results well below typical commercial pricing (which nationally runs ~254% of Medicare per RAND's hospital price transparency series). It's a working template other states — including Texas — could be pointed to when making the case for direct-contracting mandates or incentives.
- **Social angle:** Flag prominently — direct-contracting positioning. Simple bar chart: 260% cap vs. each system's actual 2026-reported performance (171%–260% range), captioned "Indiana set a price ceiling. Every hospital system beat it."

### GLP-1 makers are now selling direct to employers, cutting the PBM out of the fastest-growing line item in the drug budget
- **Source:** Eli Lilly, "Employer Connect" platform, launched March 2026; Novo Nordisk parallel direct-to-employer model announced December 2025 (reporting: Becker's Payer Issues, HR Brew, MedCity News, Fierce Healthcare)
- **Methodology/sample:** Manufacturer-published program terms and trade-press coverage of the launch; not a study, but a live commercial program change
- **The number:** Employer Connect prices any dose of Zepbound at $449/month list — the price Lilly charges network pharmacies — bypassing the PBM entirely, distributed through a network of 15+ independent program administrators (including GoodRx, Teladoc Health, Cost Plus Drugs, and Calibrate Health).
- **Why it matters:** GLP-1s already account for 11.4% of plan claims dollars (per this project's Aug. 2 finding). A manufacturer going direct-to-employer on the single biggest driver of pharmacy trend is the clearest signal yet that PBM carve-out isn't a theoretical alternative anymore — it's a live, priced commercial product competing with the traditional rebate-based channel.
- **Social angle:** Flag prominently — direct-contracting/unbundled positioning. Side-by-side: "$449 direct list price" vs. typical PBM net cost after rebates, framed as "the drugmaker just showed its hand on price."

### Million-dollar medical claims are up 46% in four years — and a single blood-cancer claim now averages $5.45M
- **Source:** Sun Life U.S., "High-Cost Claims and Injectable Drug Trends" report, published May 22, 2026
- **Methodology/sample:** Analysis of more than 70,000 high-dollar medical claims from over 3,300 self-funded employers
- **The number:** Claims of $1M or more increased in frequency 46% from 2022 to 2026. Above $3M, the biggest drivers are comorbid conditions, long inpatient stays, and injectable drugs — blood cancers (leukemia/lymphoma/myeloma) produced the highest average claims at $5.45M in 2025 (one leukemia claim hit nearly $8M), while the gene therapy Elevidys averaged $3.6M per patient and liver-disease treatment spend grew 43% year-over-year to $230K per patient.
- **Why it matters:** This is the tail-risk case for stop-loss architecture, not average trend. It's the frequency of catastrophic claims — not the headline medical trend percentage — that's pushing self-funded plans toward laddered stop-loss, aggregating deductibles, and group captives.
- **Social angle:** 2022→2026 growth-line chart (46% increase in $1M+ claims) paired with a stat card: "$5.45M — the average blood-cancer claim in 2025."

### CMS is asking, in writing, how to standardize the same price-transparency data repricing analyses run on — and stop-loss contract terms are explicitly on the list
- **Source:** CMS, CY 2027 Hospital OPPS/ASC Proposed Rule — "Request for Information: Strengthening the Standardization and Comparability of Hospital Price Transparency (HPT) Data" (published as part of the proposed rule, July 2026)
- **Methodology/sample:** Federal notice-and-comment rulemaking; RFI open to hospitals, payers/employers, data vendors, researchers, and consumers
- **The number:** Comments are due by 11:59 p.m. ET on Aug. 31, 2026 — four weeks from today. The RFI specifically asks how to standardize machine-readable-file (MRF) reporting of "outlier payments, stop-loss provisions, rate tiering, and carve-outs," alongside updates to the deemed-compliance policy for consumer price-estimator tools and the required shoppable-services list.
- **Why it matters:** The MRF data-quality gap this project has flagged before (KFF's 2025 analysis found widespread inconsistency; CMS's CY2026 rule was an earlier fix attempt) is still an open question CMS is actively soliciting input on right now — including, specifically, how stop-loss terms get reported. This is a live four-week window where a direct-contracting-focused employer or consultant voice could shape what "standardized" data looks like.
- **Social angle:** Not a chart — a call-to-action post: "The window to weigh in on how hospital price data gets standardized closes Aug. 31. Stop-loss reporting is explicitly part of the ask." Flag prominently — ties to MRF-based repricing/direct-contracting data quality.
