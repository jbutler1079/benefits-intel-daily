### FTC tells the Fourth Circuit that Amgen's patent-application deal to lock up Enbrel — pushing biosimilar entry from 2016 to 2029 — is not immune from antitrust scrutiny

- **Source:** Federal Trade Commission amicus brief in *CareFirst of Maryland, Inc. et al. v. Amgen et al.*, U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, Case No. 26-1473. Court docket shows the brief **filed 08/17/2026**; the FTC's own press release, "FTC Files Amicus Brief to Protect Competition in Biologic Drug Markets," is dated **08/20/2026** (ftc.gov, EGRESS_BLOCKED — date taken from WebSearch-indexed snippet of the release itself). Independently corroborated by [Law360: "FTC Slams Amgen's Antitrust Shield Argument At 4th Circ."](https://www.law360.com/competition/articles/2515944), [Law360: "Groups Tell 4th Circ. Not To Let Sandoz 'Relitigate' Enbrel"](https://www.law360.com/healthcare-authority/articles/2497510/groups-tell-4th-circ-not-to-let-sandoz-relitigate-enbrel), and background reporting from [BioPharma Dive: "A three-decade monopoly: how Amgen built a patent thicket around its top-selling drug"](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/amgen-enbrel-patent-thicket-monopoly-biosimilar/609042/), [Fierce Pharma: "Amgen stalls Samsung's Enbrel biosim until 2029..."](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/amgen-stalls-samsung-s-enbrel-biosim-until-2029-second-patent-win-year), and [GaBI Online: "Etanercept biosimilars delayed until 2029 in US"](https://www.gabionline.net/biosimilars/news/etanercept-biosimilars-delayed-until-2029-in-us). The brief PDF itself is indexed at ftc.gov but not fetchable this session.
- **Methodology/sample:** Not a dataset — a federal antitrust amicus brief plus the underlying litigation record and Amgen's own SEC/earnings disclosures. The FTC argues Amgen's *acquisition of Roche's pending patent applications* (later prosecuted into patents covering Enbrel/etanercept) is a private commercial transaction subject to Sherman Act Section 2 scrutiny — rejecting Amgen's position that patent-application deals are categorically shielded just because prosecuting them before the USPTO is protected petitioning activity.
- **The number:** Enbrel's original patent was set to expire in **2012**; absent the disputed patent-application acquisition, biosimilar competition could have started as early as **2016**. Instead, courts have upheld the resulting patents through **2029** — a **13-year** delay in competition on a single drug. Enbrel has generated **more than $70 billion** in cumulative revenue (on a trajectory to exceed **$100 billion**), with **$3.29 billion** in US sales in 2024 alone (Amgen 2024 annual report). Separately, I-MAK's 2024 analysis put the *combined* cost of delayed generic/biosimilar entry across just three drugs — Humira, Eliquis, and Enbrel — at **$167 billion** versus a scenario of on-time entry.
- **Why it matters:** This is a live, escalating federal case over exactly the anti-competitive patent tactics that keep specialty/biologic drug spend elevated for self-funded plans. Enbrel remains a top rheumatoid-arthritis biologic with zero US biosimilar competition 14 years after its original patent should have expired. A win for the FTC's position would set Fourth Circuit precedent limiting "patent-application acquisition" thickets — a tactic not unique to Enbrel — which directly supports specialty-drug cost-containment and PBM/manufacturer-accountability arguments employers make when evaluating direct-contracting and unbundled specialty-pharmacy strategies.
- **Social angle:** A simple timeline graphic — 2012 (patent should have expired) → 2016 (biosimilars could have entered) → 2029 (actual earliest entry) — captioned "$70 billion later, the FTC says this patent trick shouldn't be legal." Pairs well as a "why specialty drug trend keeps climbing" companion post to yesterday's Aon 9.5%/2027 cost-trend finding.
- **Caveat — flagging honestly:** ftc.gov and openmarketsinstitute.org were both EGRESS_BLOCKED, so the brief's exact text and the press release's exact date could not be directly verified — relying on WebSearch-indexed snippets and five independent secondary outlets, which agree closely but show a minor date discrepancy (docket filing 08/17 vs. press release 08/20). This does **not** tie to TX-APCD or unbundled-TPA/direct-contracting positioning specifically — it's a specialty-drug/PBM-adjacent antitrust story, included because it clears the bar on specificity, dollar figures, and multi-outlet corroboration, not because it's central to HPHP's core direct-contracting pitch.
