# Daily Benefits Intelligence — 2026-08-02

### Stop-loss premiums are outrunning the claims trend they're supposed to track
- **Source:** Aegis Risk / International Foundation of Employee Benefit Plans (IFEBP), 2025 Medical Stop-Loss Premium Survey
- **Methodology/sample:** 1,268 self-funded plan sponsors, covering 1.2M+ employees and $1.2B+ in annual specific stop-loss premium
- **The number:** 2025–2026 renewal increases ranged from 8.8% (at a $100K specific deductible) to 10.1% (at $500K); the survey's longer-term premium growth trend runs 9.9%–12.1% — well above underlying medical trend (~7–8%). This is the "leveraged trend" effect: because a stop-loss carrier only pays claims above the deductible, a modest rise in total claims translates into a much larger percentage rise in the reinsurer's exposure and premium.
- **Why it matters:** Self-funded employers benchmarking renewals against headline medical trend are underestimating their real cost curve. The gap between claims trend and stop-loss premium trend is structural, not a one-year anomaly, and it compounds every renewal cycle.
- **Social angle:** Side-by-side bar chart — claims trend vs. stop-loss premium trend — with a "why your reinsurance bill grows faster than your claims" caption. Strong LinkedIn explainer for CFOs/HR finance audiences.

### Hospitals are quoting different carriers wildly different prices under the same roof — and now employers can see it
- **Source:** Turquoise Health, analysis of CMS hospital price transparency machine-readable files; CMS enforcement update coverage (HFMA, BenefitsPro, May 2026)
- **Methodology/sample:** Repricing analysis across 500 common healthcare services, comparing carrier-negotiated rates at the same facilities using post-2026-rule MRF data (which now requires actual dollar amounts, not estimates, plus facility NPIs for cross-dataset linkage)
- **The number:** Self-insured employers that shift claims from their highest-rate carrier/plan combination to the lowest-rate one at the same hospital save an average of 27% across the 500 services studied. Separately, reference-based pricing deployments built on this transparency data have documented 15%–30% savings.
- **Why it matters:** This isn't network-narrowing or provider steerage — it's proof that carrier selection alone, at the identical facility, creates double-digit price swings. That's the core evidence base for unbundling the carrier from the network and pricing directly off transparency data.
- **Social angle:** Flag prominently — direct-contracting/unbundled-TPA positioning. A single-CPT-code, multi-carrier price comparison at one hospital, framed "same building, same code, 27% different price."

### GLP-1s now eat 11.4% of plan claims dollars, up from 6.9% three years ago
- **Source:** IFEBP, "GLP-1 Drugs: 2026 Pulse Survey (U.S.)," fielded June 2026
- **Methodology/sample:** Pulse survey of roughly 300 U.S. employer-sponsored health plans on GLP-1 utilization and coverage decisions
- **The number:** GLP-1 drugs account for 11.4% of annual claims dollars in 2026, up from 6.9% in 2023 — a 65% relative increase in three years — even though only 36% of employers cover GLP-1s for both diabetes and weight management (up marginally from 34% in 2024).
- **Why it matters:** Claims share is rising faster than coverage generosity, meaning cost pressure is coming from utilization and price, not just benefit design. Self-funded employers evaluating direct-contracting or RBP strategies need a pharmacy-specific lever (manufacturer-direct channels, carve-outs) alongside facility-price fixes — GLP-1s won't be solved by hospital repricing alone.
- **Social angle:** 2023→2026 line chart of GLP-1 share of claims; thread framed as "the drug class quietly eating your plan spend."

### Three independent 2026 trend surveys converge on the same signal: this is year three of near-double-digit growth
- **Source:** Mercer (National Survey of Employer-Sponsored Health Plans / 2026 outlook), Aon (2026 U.S. Health Care Trend Report, released Sept. 10, 2025), WTW (2026 Global Medical Trends Survey)
- **Methodology/sample:** Mercer surveys thousands of U.S. employers; Aon aggregates carrier trend filings; WTW surveys medical insurers globally — three distinct data sources, not one firm's opinion repeated
- **The number:** Mercer projects +6.5% for 2026 (highest since 2010), but employers themselves estimate +9% if they take no cost-management action; Aon projects +9.5% (pushing average cost past $17,000/employee); WTW projects +9.2%, the fastest pace since at least 2011.
- **Why it matters:** Convergence across three independently-run surveys on "near-double-digit, multi-year" growth undercuts the idea that 2025–2026 was a one-time spike. For self-funded employers, that argues for structural interventions (direct contracting, RBP, stop-loss restructuring) over incremental plan-design tweaks.
- **Social angle:** Three-bar comparison chart (Mercer/Aon/WTW) with a "three firms, one signal" caption.

### Texas has been collecting statewide claims data for 4.5 years — outside researchers only got access this spring
- **Source:** UTHealth Houston Center for Health Care Data, Texas All-Payor Claims Database (TX-APCD), established under Texas HB 2090 (Sept. 2021)
- **Methodology/sample:** All-payer database from 120+ payors, covering ~60% of Texans with medical coverage (80% including pharmacy/dental); data accessible only on a secure analytics server, no raw download
- **The number:** Qualified research entity (QRE) applications opened January 2026, with approved researchers gaining actual data access in March 2026 — meaning roughly 4.5 years of statewide claims data accumulated before any outside researcher could query it.
- **Why it matters:** Employers and consultants citing "Texas cost variation" data have effectively been working from a black box until this year. Independent verification of site-of-care and pricing-variation claims specific to Texas has been data-starved, which is exactly the gap a TX-focused direct-contracting pitch should name explicitly rather than gloss over.
- **Social angle:** Flag prominently — TX-APCD data gap. Short explainer post: "Texas has had a claims database on 80% of the state since 2021. Researchers got their first look in March 2026. Here's why that gap matters for anyone citing TX cost data."
