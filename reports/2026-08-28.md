### Business Group on Health's 2026 employer survey: 2027 costs to rise 9.2%, and for the first time a majority of employers (62%) point to hospital price hikes and consolidation as a major driver

- **Source:** Business Group on Health, "2026 Employer Health Care Strategy Survey" (media briefing led by CEO Ellen Kelsay). True primary release date resolved as **August 26, 2026** — cross-corroborated across dated URLs from [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/brucejapsen/2026/08/26/as-employer-health-costs-rise-worker-cancer-cases-an-increasing-worry/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/26/employer-health-costs-predictions), [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/employers-2027-health-costs-bgh-survey/828734/), MedCity News, PLANSPONSOR, Chief Healthcare Executive, Medical Economics, and Fierce Healthcare, all dated or relative-indexed to 8/26. This resolves the ambiguity flagged in yesterday's report: the earlier "Aug 19" date was a prior-year artifact — Aug 19–20, 2025 was when the *previous edition* of this same annual survey (forecasting 2026 costs) was released, and search synthesis had conflated the two years' press cycles. The businessgrouphealth.org primary release itself could not be directly fetched (EGRESS_BLOCKED), so this is secondary-source-confirmed, not primary-fetched, though the convergence across six independently dated outlets is strong.
- **Methodology/sample:** Annual employer survey; 127 large employers representing roughly 11 million covered lives (one outlet cites 8.7 million — minor variance across secondary sources, primary methodology page not directly verifiable this session).
- **The number:** Median projected 2027 health cost trend of **9.2%**, offset to **~8%** after plan-design changes. Employers' own retrospective 2026 forecast (made a year ago) was 8.5%→7% — continuing a pattern in which actual costs have exceeded employer predictions in each of the last three years, with the gap widening each year. Cumulative cost growth since 2018 is now projected at **~76%**, more than double general inflation over the same span. Cancer is cited by **70%** of employers as the top cost driver (up from 58% last year), a fifth consecutive year at #1; pharmacy is ~25% of total spend with 95% of employers "concerned/very concerned." The new data point for this project: **62% of employers say hospital price increases are driving costs "to a great/very great extent,"** explicitly citing provider consolidation and site-of-care cost variation as causes.
- **Why it matters:** This is a national, large-employer-sample survey — not a vendor pitch — putting a specific majority-of-employers number on hospital pricing/consolidation as a cost driver, for the first time captured in this project's tracking of this survey series. Paired with the three-year pattern of employers underestimating their own trend forecasts, it builds a case that conventional network-contract cost containment is failing employers at a rate serious enough that a majority are now naming the underlying structural cause (consolidation-driven pricing power) rather than just symptoms (utilization, drug costs). That's a direct, employer-voice-sourced argument for direct-contracting/RBP as a structural fix rather than a marginal optimization.
- **Social angle:** A stat card — "62% of employers now blame hospital pricing and consolidation" — paired with a 3-year "forecast vs. actual" line chart showing the widening miss. Frame as "employers already know the diagnosis; direct contracting is the treatment they haven't tried yet." Works as a LinkedIn post now; the cancer-cost and 76%-cumulative-growth stats are separate, strong secondary posts if this one performs well.
- **Direct-contracting/RBP tie-in flag:** Yes — directly. The 62% hospital-consolidation-blame figure is the clearest employer-self-reported validation of this project's core structural argument found in this survey series to date.
