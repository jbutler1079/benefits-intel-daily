### Marsh (Mercer)'s 2026 employer survey: 2027 health benefit costs projected up 8.2%, the highest jump since 2003 — and for the first time names AI-generated claims volume and IDR abuse as measurable cost drivers alongside GLP-1s

- **Source:** Marsh (formerly Mercer), "2026 National Survey of Employer-Sponsored Health Plans" (preliminary results), released September 2, 2026.
- **Methodology/sample:** Survey of 1,800+ US employers, fielded June 10–August 10, 2026; final results (2,000+ employers) due later in 2026.
- **The number:** Total health benefit cost per employee projected to rise **8.2%** on average in 2027 — the highest increase since 2003 — *even after* employers' planned cost-cutting measures (11% without mitigation). Fifth consecutive year of elevated growth after a decade averaging ~3%/year (2026 came in at 6.7%). Of the projected 2027 growth, roughly 1 percentage point each is attributed to GLP-1 medications, **AI-enabled claims-submission software**, and abuse of the No Surprises Act **Independent Dispute Resolution (IDR) process**. Employer response: 59% plan cost-cutting benefit-design changes; two-thirds of large employers (500+ lives) plan to raise employee premium contributions.
- **Why it matters:** This is a distinct survey from the already-reported Aon (9.5%) and WTW (11.1%) 2027 trend figures, with a starker "highest since 2003" framing — and it's the first major employer-cost survey to explicitly name *vendor-side billing gamesmanship* (AI-driven claims volume, IDR abuse) as a measurable line-item cost driver, not just utilization or drug prices. That's a direct, citable data point for the argument that opaque, bundled carrier/PBM administration lets cost drivers hide in plain sight — exactly the failure mode direct-contracting/unbundled models are pitched to fix.
- **Social angle:** "Employers now say AI-generated claims and IDR abuse are moving the needle on premiums as much as GLP-1s. The system isn't just expensive — it's being gamed, and bundled plans can't see it happening." Stat-card or short LinkedIn post pairing the 8.2%/"highest since 2003" framing with the three named drivers.
- **Direct-contracting/RBP tie-in flag:** Indirect — supports the "bundled administration hides cost drivers" argument broadly; not TX-APCD-specific.

### AHA's own CMS comment letter admits hospitals spent $43B in 2025 chasing payments insurers already owed them — $18B just overturning denials, 75% of which get overturned anyway

- **Source:** American Hospital Association, formal comment letter to CMS Administrator Mehmet Oz on the CY2027 OPPS/ASC proposed rule (CMS-1850-P), including its response to the RFI on "Strengthening the Standardization and Comparability of Hospital Price Transparency Data" (docket CMS-2026-2344). Dated August 28, 2026 (comment window closed Aug 31, 2026).
- **Methodology/sample:** AHA's own analysis, citing Premier (2025) claims-administration data and a 2023 HHS-OIG study on Medicare Advantage denial-and-appeal outcomes; AHA's annual hospital administrative-staffing survey (2024 data).
- **The number:** Hospitals spent an estimated **$43 billion in 2025** trying to collect payments insurers already owed for care delivered — **$18 billion** of that specifically overturning claims denials. A 2023 HHS-OIG study found **75%** of care denials were ultimately overturned on appeal. The average hospital employs roughly 64 administrative/billing staff dedicated to these functions, **6.5%** of total hospital employment.
- **Why it matters:** This is hospitals' own trade group quantifying, in a formal federal filing, how much cost embedded in claims is pure administrative friction from the denial/appeal cycle between hospitals and insurers — money that ultimately flows into negotiated rates and gets passed to plan sponsors. It's a strong data point for the case that unbundled, direct-contracting arrangements that cut this adjudication layer out entirely can capture real, quantifiable savings rather than theoretical ones.
- **Social angle:** "Even hospitals admit it: $43B a year just fighting insurers for money already owed — and 3 out of 4 denials get overturned anyway. That's the tax bundled billing puts on every employer's premium." Strong stat-card or thread opener.
- **Direct-contracting/RBP tie-in flag:** Direct — this is the substantive resolution of the carried-forward CMS-2026-2344 watch item (see below); AHA's ask (shift standardization burden to payers, not hospitals) is itself relevant to how TX-APCD-style transparency mandates get contested.
