### Aegis Risk's 20th-year stop-loss survey: premiums jumped up to 15.9% this year, but employer interest in reference-based pricing sits at just 2%

- **Source:** Aegis Risk, "2026 Medical Stop-Loss Premium Survey" (20th year), aegisrisk.com, headline findings live as of September 4, 2026 (full detailed report still available on request "by early September").
- **Methodology/sample:** 1,378 stop-loss policies, covering over 1,400,000 employees, representing more than $1.5 billion in annual stop-loss premium — up from the 2025 (19th-year) edition's 1,268 policies / $1.2B premium.
- **The number:** Average premium of **$260.63 PEPM** at a $100K individual deductible, down to **$58.82 PEPM** at $500K. Year-over-year premium increase of **13.6% to 15.9%+** depending on deductible level — roughly 5 to 5.5 points higher than the prior year's 8.8–10.1% increase, meaning the trend itself is accelerating. Top claimant drivers cited: cancer/neoplasms (**89%** of respondents), specialty pharmacy (**57%**). On risk-mitigation tools: AI claims-management adoption rose **8% → 20%** year-over-year; captive interest sits at **20%**; **reference-based pricing interest is just 2%**; 47% of respondents use none of the listed mitigation tools at all.
- **Why it matters:** This is the largest, most current stop-loss benchmark available, and it shows employers absorbing an accelerating cost trend almost entirely through conventional levers (captives, AI-assisted claims review) while all but ignoring reference-based pricing — the exact structural fix direct-contracting/RBP models are pitched to deliver. The pain (accelerating trend) and the market gap (2% RBP interest) sit side by side in the same dataset.
- **Social angle:** Chart pairing the YoY premium-trend acceleration (8.8–10.1% → 13.6–15.9%) against the flat 2% RBP-interest line: "Stop-loss costs are compounding faster every year. The proven lever is reference-based pricing. Only 2% of employers are even considering it."
- **Direct-contracting/RBP tie-in flag:** Direct — the survey's own risk-mitigation-tool breakout is literally measuring self-funded employer interest in RBP.

### PBM-affiliated "private label" biosimilars get frozen out of their own parent company's Medicare plans — Optum's own biosimilar reached just 0.4% of UnitedHealth Part D plans

- **Source:** Drug Channels (Adam J. Fein), "The Private Label Biosimilar Paradox in Medicare Part D," published September 1, 2026, citing a JAMA Network Open analysis of ustekinumab (Stelara) biosimilar formulary coverage, early-2026 data.
- **Methodology/sample:** Formulary-coverage analysis across Medicare Part D plans of the 12 ustekinumab biosimilars currently on the market.
- **The number:** The average Part D plan covers only **2.3 of the 12** available biosimilars. CVS Health/Cordavis's own private-label biosimilar (Pyzchiva) got **0% Part D plan coverage**. Optum Rx's own private-label biosimilar (Wezlana) appeared in just **0.4% of UnitedHealth's Part D plans** — despite the vertical-integration incentive that should favor it. Mechanism cited: the 2026 IRA Part D redesign ($615 deductible, $2,100 OOP cap, manufacturer 10%/20% Discount Program obligations vs. plan catastrophic-phase exposure rising from 20% to 60%) makes even PBM-affiliated biosimilars economically unattractive to the PBM within Part D specifically.
- **Why it matters:** This directly undercuts the standard defense of PBM vertical integration — that owning the pharmacy/manufacturer relationship lets savings pass through. Here, PBMs won't even favor their own affiliated, lower-cost biosimilar when the reimbursement math doesn't work for them, which is concrete evidence that vertical integration optimizes for PBM-side economics rather than payer cost — reinforcing the case for unbundled, non-conflicted PBM arrangements.
- **Social angle:** "Even PBMs won't use their own biosimilars when the numbers don't work for them. If you're on a bundled PBM contract, ask whose numbers are actually being optimized." Strong stat-card for a PBM-conflict-of-interest post.
- **Direct-contracting/RBP tie-in flag:** Indirect — supports the broader PBM-conflict-of-interest/unbundled-contracting argument; this data point is Medicare Part D-specific, not TX-APCD.
