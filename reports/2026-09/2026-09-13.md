### A hospital system already rated D by S&P has 48 hours left to close a $40M rescue bond or fall into outright default

- **Source:** S&P Global Ratings action and EMMA/MSRB continuing-disclosure
  filings on Norman Regional Health System (Norman, OK), via Bond Buyer
  reporting dated September 8–10, 2026.
- **Methodology/sample:** Single-issuer credit-event tracking — S&P's rating
  action on Norman Regional's outstanding revenue bonds following the
  system's missed September 1, 2026 debt-service payment, plus the
  forbearance terms disclosed in the bondholders' agreement.
- **The number:** S&P downgraded Norman Regional's long-term bond rating to
  **D** after the missed September 1 payment. Bondholders' forbearance is
  conditioned on the system closing a **$40 million senior revenue bond
  issue** — to be purchased by holders of its existing debt — by
  **Tuesday, September 15, 2026**. As of this check (September 13), no
  closing has been confirmed. The forbearance agreement requires any
  technical defaults to be cured **upon** closing, meaning a miss on the
  15th leaves the system in uncured default rather than merely delayed.
- **Why it matters:** This is a live, checkable case study in provider-side
  financial fragility — the exact kind of system-level distress self-funded
  plans point to when arguing that unbundled, direct-contracting/RBP
  arrangements shift risk away from employers rather than quietly
  subsidizing a hospital's capital structure through opaque commercial
  rates. It's also a useful counter to narratives that frame all hospital
  underpayment as employer-driven — this system's distress is playing out
  in its own bond covenants, in public, on a fixed clock.
- **Social angle:** A short countdown-style post/thread: timeline graphic
  running Sept 1 (missed payment) → Sept 8 (downgrade to D) → Sept 15
  (rescue-bond deadline) — "a hospital system two days from either curing
  its default or falling into one" — with a follow-up post once the
  deadline resolves either way.
- **Direct-contracting/TX-APCD tie-in flag:** Indirect — a financially
  distressed system's commercial rate schedule is precisely the kind of
  thing claims-level price transparency (APCD-style data) is meant to
  surface before an employer commits to a network contract with it.
