quantification from the Blue Cross Blue Shield Association: hospitals'

### Blue Cross Blue Shield Association: hospitals' AI-assisted coding tools added $942 million in claims costs over three years, with "no evidence of corresponding change in care delivered"

- **Source:** Blue Cross Blue Shield Association, claims-coding analysis,
  released **September 24, 2026** (widely reported September 25–26).
- **Methodology/sample:** Analysis of Blue Cross and Blue Shield insurance
  claims from **January 2023 through December 2025**, tracking the share
  of claims billed as "medically complex" (higher-severity diagnosis-
  related groups driven by secondary/comorbid diagnoses) and comparing
  coding-intensity trends against clinical indicators (e.g., procedure-
  specific complexity rates, transfusion rates) at hospitals with the
  highest coding-intensity increases versus other hospitals. BCBSA notes
  the analysis relies on claims rather than full clinical records, so it
  cannot independently verify the legitimacy of individual diagnoses.
- **The number:** The share of claims billed as medically complex rose
  from **37% (early 2023) to 40% (late 2025)**. More than **55,000**
  additional cases were pushed into higher-severity, higher-reimbursement
  DRGs by secondary diagnoses relative to the 2023 baseline, accounting
  for **$653 million (~70%) of the $942 million total** — about **$11,000
  in excess reimbursement per complex case**. For one example procedure
  category (bowel surgery), the share billed at the highest complexity
  tier rose from **10.2% to 22.7%** over the period. Hospitals with the
  largest coding-intensity increases showed **higher** rates of certain
  comorbidity coding (e.g., anemia, 13.7% vs. 9.9% at other hospitals) but
  **lower** rates of the clinical intervention that condition would be
  expected to require (blood transfusion, 16.9% vs. 19.3%) — the gap
  BCBSA cites as evidence of a coding/treatment disconnect. More than 60%
  of hospital systems now use AI-enabled coding technologies, per the
  analysis. The American Hospital Association disputes the framing,
  countering that patients are genuinely "older and more medically
  complex," citing a ~5% rise in case-mix index from 2019–2024 and
  attributing roughly 19% of hospital expense growth to increased patient
  complexity.
- **Why it matters:** This is the first analysis in this series to put a
  specific national dollar figure on AI-driven coding-intensity creep, and
  it lands directly on the claims-audit and coding-integrity terrain where
  unbundled TPA and claims-audit vendors compete against traditional
  carrier-administered ASO arrangements. If a large share of "complexity"
  growth in hospital claims isn't matched by clinical intensity, that's a
  concrete argument for self-funded plans to demand DRG-validation audit
  rights and independent claims review — capabilities that are often
  weaker or contractually restricted under bundled carrier TPA models.
- **Social angle:** A simple before/after chart — 37% vs. 40% "medically
  complex" share, or the bowel-surgery 10.2%→22.7% jump — paired with "AI
  is billing your health plan for sicker patients. Is it treating them
  like sicker patients?" Strong hook for a post on claims-audit rights and
  DRG validation as a cost-containment lever employers should be asking
  their TPA about.

### Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker: TrumpRx beats peer-country prices on roughly half of brand-name drugs studied — but GLP-1s and biologics are among the drugs still priced far above international benchmarks

- **Source:** Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker, "How TrumpRx brand-name
  drug prices compare to other countries," published **September 24,
  2026**.
- **Methodology/sample:** Compared list prices for **32 brand-name-only
  drugs** (excluding generics/biosimilars) available on TrumpRx as of
  August 25, 2026, against average listed prices for the same drugs in
  **11 peer OECD countries** (Australia, Austria, Belgium, Canada, France,
  Germany, Japan, Netherlands, Sweden, Switzerland, UK). Prices converted
  to USD at September 9, 2026 exchange rates; not adjusted for purchasing
  power parity.
- **The number:** **17 of 32 drugs (53%)** had TrumpRx prices below the
  peer-country average — by **1% to 63%**, averaging **31% lower** — and 5
  of those had the single lowest price of any country studied. But **15 of
  32 drugs (47%)** exceeded the peer average, by an average of **262%
  higher**, and 13 of those were the single highest-priced country
  studied. Enbrel showed the largest absolute gap: **$3,354 on TrumpRx
  versus a $641 peer-country average** — a **$2,725** difference. Premarin
  Vaginal Cream cost **more than 10 times** Canada's price. Among the
  drugs priced higher on TrumpRx: **Ozempic Pen at $349 versus a $190**
  international average, plus several other GLP-1/endocrine drugs.
- **Why it matters:** TrumpRx has been positioned as a federal
  direct-pricing fix for high U.S. drug costs, but this data shows a
  genuinely mixed picture — and the drugs still priced well above
  international peers skew toward GLP-1s and biologics, the same
  specialty-drug categories already dominating employer plan trend (per
  the Sun Life and Business Group on Health findings earlier in this
  series). For employers evaluating specialty-pharmacy and
  direct-contracting alternatives, this is a citable reason not to assume
  a federal pricing channel alone closes the gap on the highest-cost drug
  categories — the case for plan-level negotiation, international
  reference pricing, or direct-to-manufacturer contracting on specialty
  drugs specifically remains intact.
- **Social angle:** A two-sided bar chart — cheaper-on-TrumpRx drugs vs.
  pricier-on-TrumpRx drugs — highlighting that GLP-1s land on the
  "pricier" side. Headline angle: "TrumpRx cut prices on half these drugs.
  The ones still overpriced are the ones costing your plan the most."
