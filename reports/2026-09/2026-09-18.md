### Hospital purchasing power has consolidated sharply into three GPOs — the top three's combined market share jumped from 62% to 84% in a decade

- **Source:** Tseng WH, Bond AM, Dean EB, "Consolidation of Hospital
  Affiliations With Group Purchasing Organizations," *JAMA Health Forum*,
  published online September 11, 2026 (Vol 7, No. 9), DOI:
  10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.3151, open access.
- **Methodology/sample:** Cross-sectional study combining the AHA Annual
  Survey Database, 340B OPAIS, and IQVIA Healthcare Organizations database.
  5,008 hospitals analyzed in 2014 vs. 4,760 in 2024, with a sensitivity
  check on the 3,877 hospitals present in both years.
- **The number:** Combined market share of the three largest group
  purchasing organizations grew from **61.7% (2014) to 84.1% (2024)**
  (64.3% → 84.5% among hospitals present in both years). Vizient alone
  nearly doubled, from 23.0% (1,152 hospitals) to **43.4%** (2,066
  hospitals). MedAssets/HealthTrust's combined ~37.0% share in 2014 fell to
  17.0% post-merger consolidation, while Premier stayed roughly flat
  (22.1% → 23.7%). Median 2024 pharmacy expense varies sharply by GPO
  affiliation: **$7.98M** at Vizient-affiliated hospitals vs. **$1.4M** at
  non-GPO-affiliated hospitals.
- **Why it matters:** This is a supply-side consolidation story distinct
  from the hospital-system-M&A narrative employers usually hear about — it
  documents monopsony-like concentration in the purchasing layer (devices,
  drugs, supplies) that sits upstream of what hospitals ultimately charge
  payers. The authors explicitly flag reduced cost-containment incentive as
  a transparency-monitoring risk, reinforcing that hospital cost structure
  is opaque and consolidating at nearly every layer, not just the
  provider-market layer self-funded plans already watch.
- **Social angle:** A simple stacked-share chart, 2014 vs. 2024, three GPOs
  eating the rest of the market — captioned "hospitals don't just have
  pricing power over you, their suppliers have it over them."
- **Direct-contracting/TX-APCD tie-in flag:** Indirect — strengthens the
  general "cost structure is opaque and consolidating everywhere" argument
  underlying direct-contracting and all-payer claims transparency pitches,
  but doesn't touch TX-APCD specifically.

### The 8th PatientRightsAdvocate.org compliance scorecard confirms the "compliant ≠ usable" gap, now with a specific enforcement mechanism behind the jump

- **Source:** PatientRightsAdvocate.org, 8th Semi-Annual Hospital Price
  Transparency Report; covered by Medical Daily (Sept 14, 2026) and
  Healthcare Dive's ongoing series coverage.
- **Methodology/sample:** Nonprofit advocacy-group scorecard (explicitly not
  a federal audit) reviewing 2,000 U.S. hospitals against CMS's Hospital
  Price Transparency Rule; 8th report since PRA began tracking in 2021.
- **The number:** National full compliance is **49.4%**, up from 21% in the
  prior report — the highest since tracking began in 2021. Only **18%** of
  reviewed hospitals post actual dollar-and-cents prices for at least half
  their items/services rather than gating behind estimator tools. CMS has
  now fined **28 hospitals** (up from 15), with corrective-action warnings
  sent to **500+ hospitals** in June 2026 and penalties up to $2M/year for
  repeat noncompliance.
- **Why it matters:** PRA credits the jump largely to CMS's CY2026 OPPS/ASC
  technical-specification updates (effective Jan 1, 2026) with enforcement
  starting April 1, 2026 — i.e., there's now a specific regulatory mechanism
  behind the compliance rise, not just voluntary improvement. That still
  leaves 82% of "compliant" files not delivering real dollar-and-cents
  prices, which is the concrete data-quality argument for why employers need
  independent claims-based benchmarks (RAND, TX-APCD, Sage) rather than
  trusting hospital-published files. This closely tracks and updates the
  same PRA report cited in yesterday's finding — new here is the specific
  CMS enforcement mechanism (CY2026 OPPS/ASC rule) driving it.
- **Social angle:** "49% compliant. 18% actually show you a price." — same
  split-bar framing as before, now captioned with the enforcement trigger:
  "here's the rule that moved the number."
- **Direct-contracting/TX-APCD tie-in flag:** Direct — reinforces the
  "don't trust the hospital's own file, get independent benchmark data"
  argument central to the TX-APCD data-gap narrative.
