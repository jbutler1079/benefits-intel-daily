### Real-world test of the GLP-1 coverage retreat: Cigna, PepsiCo, and Starbucks have each cut their own employees' weight-loss drug benefit — and Cigna employees are pushing back

- **Source:** Bloomberg, "Pepsi, Starbucks, Cigna Among Companies Dropping
  GLP-1 Insurance Benefits," published **September 23, 2026**. Corroborated
  by HR Executive/WFSB reporting on the Cigna employee petition (dated
  September 2–7, 2026), Becker's Payer Issues, and Claims Journal's
  reporting on PepsiCo's announcement (dated **August 26, 2026**).
  Underlying survey context: Business Group on Health, *2027 Employer
  Healthcare Strategy Survey* (127 large employers, ~8.7 million covered
  lives), already reported in this series September 21–22.
- **Methodology/sample:** Not a survey — direct corporate policy actions at
  three large, self-insured employers, reported via company communications
  to employees and confirmed press coverage. Cigna's health plan (self-
  administered, covering 50,000+ of its own U.S. employees) ended GLP-1
  weight-loss coverage **July 1, 2026**; PepsiCo ended its own employees'
  weight-loss coverage (retaining coverage for diabetes use) effective
  **October 2026**; Starbucks is ending weight-loss coverage starting
  **October 2026**.
- **The number:** More than **450 Cigna employees** signed an internal
  petition after the cutoff. One employee's monthly out-of-pocket cost for
  her GLP-1 prescription rose from **$25 to roughly $500** once Cigna's
  plan stopped covering it. Cigna's leadership declined to reverse the
  decision after receiving the petition in early July. Separately, per
  Claims Journal's PepsiCo coverage citing broader market data, **6% of
  large employers** had already dropped GLP-1 weight-loss coverage in 2026,
  with another **5%** planning to drop it in 2027 (consistent with BGH's
  previously reported 14%-by-2027 figure, which combines both cohorts).
- **Why it matters:** This is the coverage-retreat story moving from
  survey intentions to executed plan-design decisions at recognizable
  self-insured employers — including an insurer cutting its *own* plan.
  For employers and brokers still weighing GLP-1 coverage, it's evidence
  that even large, sophisticated plan sponsors are concluding the
  cost trajectory isn't sustainable at current formulary terms, and that
  doing so carries real internal friction (the Cigna petition) worth
  planning for — clinical eligibility gates, weight-management program
  requirements, or narrower formulary access may be a less disruptive
  middle path than an outright drop.
- **Social angle:** "An insurance company just stopped covering weight-loss
  drugs for its own employees. One worker's cost went from $25 to $500 a
  month overnight — and 450+ of her colleagues signed a petition asking
  the company to reverse it. Cigna said no. If your plan is still deciding
  on GLP-1 coverage, this is what 'no' looks like in practice." Strong as
  a LinkedIn post or short case-study thread on plan-design middle-ground
  options (eligibility gates vs. outright removal).

### Texas leads the nation in private-equity hospital ownership — 92 PE-owned hospitals, 20.3% of the state's private hospitals, per a new national tracker

- **Source:** Private Equity Stakeholder Project (PESP), *Private Equity
  Hospital Tracker 2026*, published **July 20, 2026**. **Date flag:
  published roughly two months before this report's window; included
  because it has not previously run in this series and directly surfaces a
  Texas-specific data point this report otherwise had nothing fresh for
  today.**
- **Methodology/sample:** PESP identified PE-owned hospitals using CMS
  Medicare-enrolled hospital lists, the CMS "Hospital All Owners
  Information" database, Pitchbook deal data, and news searches — an
  approximation, per PESP's own methodology note, based on the best
  available public data, covering all U.S. hospitals with identifiable PE
  ownership.
- **The number:** **447 U.S. hospitals** are PE-owned — **9.5%** of all
  private hospitals and **26.8%** of proprietary for-profit hospitals.
  **Texas leads every state with 92 PE-owned hospitals**, representing
  **20.3%** of the state's private hospitals (New Mexico has the highest
  *proportion* at 42.1%, off a much smaller base). Nationally, **30.4%**
  of PE-owned hospitals have their real estate held by a separate REIT —
  nearly triple the rate for all for-profit hospitals (11.9%) — and
  **21.4%** operate through joint ventures with nonprofit health systems.
  Apollo Global Management, through Lifepoint Health and ScionHealth,
  controls **203-plus** hospital locations, by far the largest single PE
  owner.
- **Why it matters:** Texas-specific hospital-ownership concentration data
  is directly relevant to any Texas-market direct-contracting or
  reference-based-pricing pitch — a fifth of the state's private hospitals
  sit inside PE-owned systems whose financing structures (sale-leasebacks,
  REIT-separated real estate, dividend recapitalizations, layered joint
  ventures) create balance-sheet incentives that can run counter to
  straightforward unit-price negotiation. It also matters for the
  TX-APCD conversation: PE-owned systems' complex, often opaque corporate
  structures are part of why claims- and cost-based data infrastructure —
  the exact kind TX-APCD is supposed to provide and is currently failing
  to deliver on schedule (see watch items) — matters more, not less, in
  Texas specifically.
- **Social angle:** Texas map/bar chart of PE-owned hospitals by state:
  "1 in 5 private hospitals in Texas is now owned by a private equity
  firm — the highest hospital count of any state. Nearly a third of these
  deals split off the real estate into a REIT. If you're negotiating rates
  in Texas, know who actually owns the building you're negotiating with."
  Strong for a Texas-focused LinkedIn post tied to the HPHP go-to-market.
