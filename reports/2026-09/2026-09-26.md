# Daily Benefits Intelligence — 2026-09-26

Two findings cleared the bar today, both pointing at where the "savings"
employers expect from cost-containment levers actually go. The lead item
is a specialty-pharmacy supply-chain finding from Drug Channels Institute:
biosimilars — the drugs plan sponsors are told to adopt for savings — have
become the "Big Three" wholesalers' fastest-growing and most profitable
product category, with gross profit from biosimilar distribution nearly
tripling from $1.4 billion (2022) to over $4 billion (2026) even as unit
volumes shift almost entirely to biosimilar versions. The second finding
is Business Group on Health's 2027 Employer Healthcare Strategy Survey,
which names cancer — not GLP-1s — as employers' top cost driver for the
first time (70% of respondents, up from 58% in 2025) and puts cumulative
health-cost growth at 76% over the past decade, roughly double general
inflation. On watch items: Norman Regional Health System's $40 million

September 26 — eleven days past target with the health system still

### Drug Channels: biosimilars are now the "Big Three" wholesalers' fastest-growing profit center — gross profit nearly tripled to over $4 billion in four years, even as unit volumes shifted almost entirely away from brand-name reference products

- **Source:** Drug Channels Institute (Adam Fein), "The Spinoff: How
  Biosimilars Are Becoming Wholesalers' Next Hit Show," published
  **September 22, 2026**.
- **Methodology/sample:** Analysis of the "Big Three" pharmaceutical
  wholesalers' (AmerisourceBergen/Cencora, Cardinal Health, McKesson)
  drug distribution revenue and gross profit by product category,
  2022–2026, drawing on wholesaler financial filings and Drug Channels'
  ongoing biosimilar unit-share tracking for selected reference products.
- **The number:** Biosimilars accounted for just **6% of wholesalers'
  drug distribution revenues** in 2026 but **23% of their total gross
  profits** — a margin concentration mirrored in unit economics, where
  provider-administered biosimilars carry an **8.2% gross margin** versus
  **12.5% for generics**. Biosimilar gross profit at the Big Three grew
  from **$1.4 billion in 2022 to more than $4 billion in 2026**,
  accounting for roughly **70% of the Big Three's total gross-profit
  growth** over that period (their combined drug-distribution gross
  profit rose from $15.9 billion to $20.2 billion). Meanwhile generic
  drugs' share of wholesaler profit fell from **68% (2022) to 52%
  (2026)**, and biosimilar unit share for selected reference products
  jumped to **61%–93%**, up from **15%–68% in 2020**.
- **Why it matters:** Biosimilar adoption is typically pitched to
  self-funded employers as a straightforward drug-trend lever — same
  clinical outcome, lower list price. This data shows the supply chain
  has repriced around that shift: as biosimilar competition drives down
  what wholesalers earn on generics, wholesalers are extracting rising
  margins from the biosimilar category itself, even as brand reference
  products lose volume almost entirely. For employers and TPAs auditing
  where pharmacy-trend savings are supposed to show up, this is a
  citable reason to ask whether wholesaler and distribution markups —
  not just PBM rebates and spread pricing — are eating into the expected
  biosimilar savings, and to push for distribution-fee transparency in
  PBM and specialty-pharmacy contracts alongside the more commonly
  scrutinized rebate terms.
- **Social angle:** A simple "where did the biosimilar savings go" bar
  chart — wholesaler gross profit from biosimilars, 2022 ($1.4B) vs. 2026
  ($4B+) — paired with the line "biosimilars were supposed to save money.
  They did — for wholesalers." Strong LinkedIn post connecting PBM/supply
  -chain transparency advocacy to a concrete, sourced number.

### Business Group on Health's 2027 Employer Healthcare Strategy Survey: cancer overtakes GLP-1s as employers' #1 cost driver, and cumulative health-cost growth hits 76% over the past decade

- **Source:** Business Group on Health, "2027 Employer Healthcare
  Strategy Survey" (full report and executive summary), published
  **August 26, 2026**. **Date flag: published roughly four weeks before
  this report's window; included because it has not previously run in
  this series and its cancer-cost-driver and decade-cumulative-growth
  figures are new data points distinct from the Mercer 8.2% trend figure
  already covered September 25.**
- **Methodology/sample:** Survey of **127 large employers** covering
  approximately **8.7 million** covered Americans, fielded **June 2026**.
- **The number:** **70% of employers named cancer their #1 healthcare
  cost driver for 2026**, up from **58% in 2025**, with **92% ranking it
  among their top three cost drivers**. Employers project a **median 9.2%
  increase** in 2027 health costs (8% after planned plan-design changes),
  and calculate that **cumulative health-benefit costs have risen 76%
  over the past decade** — roughly double the U.S. general inflation
  rate over the same period. Employer-sponsored **GLP-1 coverage for
  weight loss dropped from 72% to 60%** year-over-year, with **not a
  single surveyed employer** planning to newly add GLP-1 coverage in
  2027 — corroborating, from an independent employer-coalition source,
  the same GLP-1 pullback Mercer/Cigna/PepsiCo/Starbucks data already
  documented in this series. On the response side, **92% of employers**
  report using at least one alternative payment or delivery-system
  strategy, **95%** have issued an RFP for at least one vendor category
  in the past year, and **58%** have already replaced or plan to replace
  an underperforming vendor.
- **Why it matters:** This is the first primary employer-coalition data
  point in this series naming cancer — not pharmacy, not GLP-1s — as the
  dominant, and rapidly rising, cost driver, which reframes the
  site-of-care and vendor-scrutiny conversation: cancer care concentrates
  spend in exactly the high-cost hospital-outpatient and specialty-drug
  settings where reference-based pricing, centers-of-excellence
  contracting, and unbundled specialty-pharmacy carve-outs have the most
  leverage. The near-universal vendor RFP/replacement activity (95%/58%)
  is itself a citable data point for employers weighing whether their
  current TPA or PBM has actually been benchmarked against alternatives.
- **Social angle:** "Cancer just passed GLP-1s as the #1 reason your
  health plan costs are rising." A chart tracking the 58%→70% jump is a
  strong hook for a post pivoting the audience from the GLP-1 cost
  conversation toward oncology site-of-care and specialty-drug
  contracting — a less crowded, differentiated angle for HPHP's
  messaging.
