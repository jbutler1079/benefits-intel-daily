### NCQA's 2026 Health Plan Ratings: five-star plans jump 64% year-over-year — but the quality gains cluster in behavioral health and chronic disease management

- **Source:** NCQA, 2026 Health Plan Ratings, published September 15, 2026
  (ncqa.org/news/ncqa-unveils-2026-health-plan-ratings/).
- **Methodology/sample:** NCQA's annual 0–5 (half-point) star scale built on
  HEDIS MY2025 clinical-quality data, CAHPS member-experience survey results,
  and accreditation status. Covers commercial, Medicare Advantage, and
  Medicaid plans representing roughly 236 million enrollees nationally.
- **The number:** **18 plans** earned the top 5-star rating — 9 commercial, 5
  Medicare Advantage, 4 Medicaid — up from **11** in 2025, a **64% increase**.
  A further **53 plans** earned 4.5 stars. **22 of 28** chronic-disease
  measures improved year-over-year; Follow-Up After Hospitalization for
  Mental Illness rose **8.9 points** (commercial), **9.0 points** (Medicaid),
  and **11.9 points** (Medicare Advantage); Kidney Health Evaluation for
  Diabetes rose **7.3 points** in Medicare Advantage. Medicaid plans improved
  on 89% of measures year-over-year.
- **Why it matters:** Gives self-funded plan sponsors a fresh, dated quality
  benchmark right as several major purchasers (CalPERS, Washington PEBB) are
  mid-open-enrollment — useful for steering enrollees toward higher-rated
  commercial networks and for holding narrow-network or direct-contract
  partners to a measurable national quality bar rather than price alone.
- **Social angle:** Before/after bar chart: 11 → 18 five-star plans (+64%),
  captioned with the "22 of 28 chronic-disease measures improved" stat as
  supporting proof the gain isn't a one-off.
- **Direct-contracting/TX-APCD tie-in flag:** Indirect — a quality
  complement to the price-transparency conversation; useful to pair with
  cost data when pitching direct-contracting arrangements that also carry
  quality guarantees, rather than positioning them as pure cost plays.
- **Caveat:** The full plan-by-plan list and complete star-distribution table
  at reportcards.ncqa.org remains JS-rendered and could not be independently
  cross-verified; the numbers above come from NCQA's own news release, which
  is authoritative but single-sourced.

### A hospital that defaulted on $61M in bonds just emerged from Chapter 11 — with the state picking up $40M of the tab

- **Source:** WSFA and WAKA 8 (Montgomery, AL local news), September 14–15,
  2026, reporting Jackson Hospital & Clinic's own announcement.
- **Methodology/sample:** N/A — single-issuer corporate bankruptcy/disclosure
  event, not a dataset or study.
- **The number:** Jackson Hospital & Clinic defaulted on **$61 million** in
  bonds in September 2024, filed Chapter 11 in February 2025, and announced
  its emergence from bankruptcy on **September 14, 2026** — backed by a
  **$40 million** commitment from Alabama Governor Kay Ivey plus unspecified
  additional city/county contributions. The hospital's CEO said the exit
  unfreezes previously-stalled physician recruitment and service expansion.
- **Why it matters:** A real-world precedent for how a distressed nonprofit
  hospital's bond default actually resolves — a direct comp for Norman
  Regional Health System's own unresolved $40M rescue financing (see watch
  items below), and a concrete data point for counterparty/network-financial-
  health screening in self-funded plan design.
- **Social angle:** Side-by-side case-study post: two hospitals, two $40M
  rescue numbers, one resolved with state backing (Jackson, AL), one still
  pending past its own deadline (Norman, OK) — a natural "which way does this
  go" thread.
- **Direct-contracting/TX-APCD tie-in flag:** Indirect — not Texas-specific,
  but reinforces the broader argument that network financial-health
  screening (debt, reserves, state/local backstop risk) belongs in
  self-funded plan design, the same argument APCD/990/EMMA data exists to
  support.
