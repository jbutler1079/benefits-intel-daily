### Hospital price-transparency compliance more than doubled to 49% — but only 18% of "compliant" hospitals post real dollar prices

- **Source:** PatientRightsAdvocate.org (PRA), Eighth Hospital Price
  Transparency Compliance Report, national release ~September 10, 2026, with
  state-level cuts and press pickups continuing through September 14–17,
  2026 (Wisconsin cut published Sept 14; Indiana cut covered Sept 16).
- **Methodology/sample:** PRA reviewed a sample of roughly 2,000 U.S.
  hospitals against CMS's federal Hospital Price Transparency Rule (45 CFR
  180) requirements for machine-readable files and shoppable-service
  displays.
- **The number:** National full compliance is now **49.4%**, up from **21%**
  in PRA's prior (November 2024) report — more than double. But only **18%**
  of hospitals post actual dollar-and-cents prices for at least half their
  items/services; most "compliant" files still gate real prices behind
  estimator tools requiring extra inputs. CMS enforcement: **28 hospitals**
  fined (up from 15 previously), with warning/corrective-action letters sent
  to **500+ hospitals** in June 2026 and penalties up to $2M/year possible.
  State cuts show the compliance-methodology fight is intensifying:
  Wisconsin (61% compliant per PRA, 46 hospitals reviewed) had its hospital
  association publicly dispute PRA's methodology on the record; Indiana
  jumped to 70% compliant from 11% in late 2024.
- **Why it matters:** The 49% headline is real progress on the mandate that
  self-funded plans and RBP/direct-contracting consultants depend on for
  usable hospital pricing — but the 18% "real dollar prices" figure is the
  more important number for the pitch: even "compliant" files are frequently
  not decision-useful on their own, which is exactly the gap that
  independent claims-based benchmarks (RAND, NASHP, HCCI, state APCDs) exist
  to fill.
- **Social angle:** Split-bar chart, "49% compliant" vs. "18% show real
  prices" — the visual gap between the headline compliance number and the
  usability number is the whole story, captioned "compliant ≠ usable."
- **Direct-contracting/TX-APCD tie-in flag:** Direct — reinforces the "don't
  trust the hospital's own published file, get independent benchmark data"
  argument underlying RBP/direct-contracting pitches, and the state-level
  methodology disputes (Wisconsin vs. PRA) are a useful parallel to watch
  against Texas's own APCD data-gap fight.

### A new broker survey finds 76% of books of business have shifted toward self-funding — and a wide gap between brokers' own fiduciary anxiety and what clients are actually asking about

- **Source:** The Phia Group, LLC, "2026 Broker Survey," published via press
  release (PR Newswire), September 17, 2026.
- **Methodology/sample:** 124 broker/advisor submissions; multi-select
  questions, so totals can exceed 100%.
- **The number:** **76%** of brokers report their book of business has
  shifted toward self-funding over the past 12 months. **78%** of brokers
  cite fiduciary oversight as a top personal concern, but only **22%** say
  clients are actually asking about it. **78%** report no working visibility
  into No Surprises Act/IDR performance; **62%** describe client visibility
  into subrogation/recovery as "limited" or "very little," with only **4%**
  reporting strong visibility into recovery outcomes. **70%** report most
  clients expecting or already seeing double-digit 2026 renewal increases.
  Pharmacy/PBM/specialty drug costs were flagged by 16–18% as the biggest or
  most underestimated cost pressure.
- **Why it matters:** This is a dated, primary-source data point quantifying
  the continued broker-side migration to self-funding (76%) plus documented,
  citable evidence of the "black box" administration problem — NSA/IDR and
  subrogation visibility gaps — that unbundled/independent fiduciary and
  claims-audit services are built to solve. The 78%-vs-22% disconnect is a
  ready-made stat for making the case to employers that their broker is more
  worried about fiduciary exposure than the employer realizes.
- **Social angle:** "78% of brokers are worried about fiduciary risk. Only
  22% of their clients are asking about it." — a two-line contrast post or
  the opening slide of a fiduciary-checklist lead-gen piece (54% of surveyed
  brokers said a fiduciary checklist would help them advise clients better).
- **Direct-contracting/TX-APCD tie-in flag:** Indirect — supports the
  unbundled-fiduciary/vendor-oversight narrative broadly (NSA/IDR and
  subrogation opacity are the same category of bundled-TPA blind spot that
  direct-contracting and carve-out audit models are pitched to fix); no
  direct TX-APCD tie.

### A bipartisan House bill would force PBMs to disclose their "net effective cost" across Medicare, Medicare Advantage, and employer plans alike

- **Source:** Rep. Lucy McBath (GA-06), "Net Effective Cost Transparency and
  Prescription Drug Affordability Act of 2026," introduced September 15,
  2026 (mcbath.house.gov press release), co-led with Reps. Mackenzie (PA-07),
  Auchincloss (MA-04), Allen (GA-12), and Miller (WV-01).
- **Methodology/sample:** N/A — federal legislation, not a dataset or study.
- **The number:** The bill would mandate disclosure of PBMs' "net effective
  cost" — the true price after rebates, discounts, and fees — to allow
  apples-to-apples comparison of PBM bids across Medicare, Medicare
  Advantage, and employer-sponsored plans. The release specifies no dollar
  figures or penalty amounts; it is a bill introduction, not enacted law or
  a finalized rule.
- **Why it matters:** If it advances, this would give self-funded plan
  sponsors a standardized, legally-mandated apples-to-apples PBM comparison
  metric — directly relevant to plan sponsors currently struggling to
  compare PBM bids on anything other than headline rebate guarantees. It
  lands the same week the EBSA PBM fee-disclosure rule (RIN 1210-AB37)
  remains stalled and the FTC's Caremark/Zinc consent order remains
  unvoted — three separate federal PBM-transparency tracks moving (or not
  moving) in parallel.
- **Social angle:** A short explainer post on "net effective cost" as a
  plain-English translation of what PBM rebate opacity actually costs
  employers — timely alongside the stalled EBSA rule as a "here's what
  Congress is trying to do instead" angle.
- **Direct-contracting/TX-APCD tie-in flag:** Indirect — reinforces the
  broader PBM-transparency/unbundled-pharmacy-benefit argument; not
  Texas-specific.
