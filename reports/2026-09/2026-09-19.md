### RAND-affiliated study documents the exact mechanism TPAs use to hide fees from self-funded employers — including a "$30,000 of $31,500 was a fee" quote

- **Source:** Buttorff C, Rasmussen PW, Bhandarkar M, Shiferaw M, Duffy EL,
  "Paying in the Dark: Third-Party Administrator Fees and the Limits of
  Employer Cost Control in Self-Insured Health Plans," *Health Affairs
  Scholar*, Vol. 4, Issue 8, qxag177, published online July 23, 2026
  (August 2026 issue). DOI: 10.1093/haschl/qxag177. Open access, also
  cross-posted by RAND. **Date flag: published ~8 weeks before this
  report's window. Surfaced now because it is an unusually precise, direct
  hit on the unbundled-TPA narrative and no evidence was found that it has
  previously run as a headline item in this report series — treat as a
  catch of an earlier miss, not breaking news.**
- **Methodology/sample:** Qualitative key-informant study, 22
  semi-structured interviews covering 34 participants across four
  stakeholder groups (employers/purchaser coalitions; TPAs and partners;
  consultants/brokers; legal/policy experts), fielded fall 2025.
  RAND-affiliated authorship; explicitly not a claims-based quantitative
  study, and the authors flag that limitation themselves.
- **The number:** No single aggregate dollar figure (qualitative study by
  design), but a documented "layered fee architecture" beyond the base
  admin PEPM fee: out-of-network "shared savings" repricing fees (one
  interviewee describes a TPA negotiating a bill down to $1,500, then
  billing the plan **$31,500, of which $30,000 is a fee**), overpayment-
  recovery fees (sometimes on errors the TPA itself caused), retained
  network-access fees (3–5%, undisclosed indirect compensation from
  leased carrier networks), cross-plan offsetting, and a-la-carte charges
  for data/audit access. The study also notes CAA 2026 amendments extend
  TPA/PBM compensation-disclosure requirements — but not until **2028**.
- **Why it matters:** This is peer-reviewed, RAND-branded empirical
  documentation — not a vendor pitch deck — of precisely the opacity
  mechanism that unbundled TPA and direct-contracting models exist to
  eliminate. The specific "$30,000 of $31,500 is a fee" example is a rare
  case where an academic study produces a genuinely quotable, visceral
  number rather than an abstract percentage.
- **Social angle:** "RAND just published the receipts: a TPA negotiates
  your bill down, then bills you 95% of the 'savings' as its own fee.
  This is exactly why unbundling the TPA relationship matters." Good as a
  quote-card post, or a short thread walking through each fee type named
  in the study.
- **Direct-contracting/TX-APCD tie-in flag:** Direct — core unbundled-TPA
  positioning material; does not touch TX-APCD specifically.

### Federal court blocks Illinois PBM reporting mandate for self-funded plans on ERISA preemption grounds

- **Source:** *Pharmaceutical Care Management Association v. Gillespie*,
  No. 26-cv-3200, 2026 WL 2569468 (C.D. Ill. Aug 31, 2026), Judge Colleen
  R. Lawless. PCMA press release; coverage in Bloomberg Law and
  Healthcare Dive. **Date flag: ruling issued ~3 weeks before this
  report's window; included because it is a live, current-year legal
  precedent not found in any prior report in this series.**
- **Methodology/sample:** N/A — federal court ruling. Preliminary
  injunction, not yet a final merits decision.
- **The number:** The court found PCMA (the PBM trade association) likely
  to succeed on the merits of its ERISA-preemption claim and granted a
  preliminary injunction blocking Illinois's Prescription Drug
  Affordability Act reporting mandates as applied to PBMs serving
  self-insured ERISA plans.
- **Why it matters:** This is a concrete, current-year reaffirmation that
  ERISA preemption still shields self-funded plans (and their PBM
  vendors) from at least some state-level transparency mandates — a
  useful, checkable counterpoint to the broader "states are closing the
  transparency gap" narrative this report otherwise tracks (Indiana HEA
  1004, TX-APCD, Sage Transparency). It's directly relevant to the "why
  self-fund" pitch: plan sponsors get a real regulatory carve-out that
  fully-insured employers in the same state don't.
- **Social angle:** "A federal judge just reminded everyone: ERISA still
  preempts state PBM reporting laws for self-funded plans. If you're
  weighing self-funding, know which state mandates actually apply to
  you — and which don't."
- **Direct-contracting/TX-APCD tie-in flag:** Direct, to the
  self-funded/ERISA regulatory-insulation argument generally — not
  TX-APCD specifically, and note the tension it creates with this
  report's usual "more transparency mandates are coming" framing, which
  is itself worth naming explicitly in any social content drawn from
  this item.
