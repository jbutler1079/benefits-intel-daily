### Ionis prices the first-ever Alexander disease treatment at $285,000 a dose — $1.14M a year for roughly 300 U.S. patients

- **Source:** Ionis Pharmaceuticals, FDA approval and commercial-launch coverage for ZANVASTRO (zilganersen), announced September 3–4, 2026 (pricing detail via BioWorld/StockTitan launch coverage; Ionis's own press release did not disclose price at time of approval).
- **Methodology/sample:** N/A — single-product commercial list-price disclosure, not a study. Alexander disease is ultra-rare (estimated incidence of 1 in 1–3 million people worldwide); Ionis's Chief Global Product Strategy Officer put the diagnosed U.S. population at roughly 300 patients.
- **The number:** **$285,000 per dose**, administered quarterly as an intrathecal injection — **$1.14 million per year** before rebates or discounts. A William Blair analyst projects only ~$160M in peak U.S. sales by 2040, reflecting how small the treatable population is even at that price.
- **Why it matters:** This lands the same week yesterday's Aegis Risk stop-loss survey finding (logged Sept 4) identified specialty pharmacy (57% of respondents) and cancer/neoplasms (89%) as the top catastrophic-claim drivers pushing stop-loss premiums up 13.6–15.9% year-over-year. A single Alexander disease diagnosis in a self-funded plan is now a $1.14M/year exposure — exactly the kind of ultra-high-cost outlier claim that stop-loss reinsurance and specialty-drug carve-out strategies exist to manage, and a concrete number to cite when making that case to employers.
- **Social angle:** "One diagnosis, $1.14M a year. Self-funded plans without the right stop-loss and specialty-carve-out structure are one ultra-rare disease claim away from a catastrophic year." Pairs well as a follow-on to the Aegis stop-loss stat-card.
- **Direct-contracting/RBP tie-in flag:** Indirect — illustrates specialty-drug tail-risk exposure that stop-loss/specialty-carve-out strategies address; not TX-APCD-specific.
