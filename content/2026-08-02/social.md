## Post 1 — Stop-loss leveraged trend (LinkedIn)

Your claims are up ~7–8%. Your stop-loss renewal is up ~10%. That gap isn't a mistake — it's math.

The 2025 Aegis Risk / IFEBP Medical Stop-Loss Premium Survey (1,268 plan sponsors, $1.2B+ in specific premium) pegs 2025–2026 renewals at 8.8% (at a $100K deductible) to 10.1% (at $500K), with the longer-term trend running 9.9%–12.1%.

Why faster than claims? A stop-loss carrier only pays claims ABOVE your deductible. So a modest rise in total claims lands as a much bigger percentage rise in the reinsurer's exposure — the "leveraged trend" effect. It compounds every renewal.

If you're benchmarking your reinsurance bill against headline medical trend, you're underestimating your real cost curve.

## Post 2 — Same building, same code, 27% different price ⚑ DIRECT-CONTRACTING

Same hospital. Same procedure code. Two carriers. A 27% price difference.

Turquoise Health's repricing analysis of CMS hospital price-transparency files (500 common services, using post-2026-rule data with actual dollar amounts + facility NPIs) found self-insured employers save an average of 27% by moving claims from their highest-rate carrier/plan to the lowest-rate one — AT THE SAME FACILITY. Reference-based pricing built on this data has documented 15%–30% savings.

This isn't network-narrowing or steering patients elsewhere. It's proof that the carrier you bolt onto a network — not the network itself — is driving double-digit price swings. That's the whole case for unbundling.

## Post 3 — GLP-1s now 11.4% of plan claims (X/thread)

GLP-1 drugs are now 11.4% of employer plan claims dollars — up from 6.9% in 2023. A 65% jump in three years.

(IFEBP GLP-1 2026 Pulse Survey, ~300 U.S. employer plans, fielded June 2026.)

The kicker: only 36% of employers cover GLP-1s for both diabetes AND weight management (up barely from 34% in 2024). So claims share is climbing faster than coverage generosity — the pressure is utilization and price, not benefit design.

Takeaway for self-funded plans: hospital repricing won't fix this one. You need a pharmacy-specific lever — manufacturer-direct channels, carve-outs — running alongside your facility-price strategy.

## Post 4 — Three surveys, one signal (LinkedIn)

2025–2026 wasn't a one-time spike. Three independently-run surveys land on the same story: multi-year, near-double-digit medical cost growth.

- Mercer: +6.5% projected for 2026 (highest since 2010) — but employers estimate +9% if they do nothing.
- Aon: +9.5%, pushing average cost past $17,000/employee.
- WTW: +9.2%, the fastest pace since at least 2011.

Three different data sources — an employer survey, aggregated carrier filings, and a global insurer survey — not one firm's opinion echoed three times.

When the signal converges like this, incremental plan-design tweaks stop being enough. It's an argument for structural moves: direct contracting, reference-based pricing, stop-loss restructuring.

## Post 5 — Texas's 4.5-year data black box ⚑ TX-APCD GAP

Texas has been collecting statewide claims data on ~80% of the state since 2021. Outside researchers got their first look in March 2026.

The TX All-Payor Claims Database (UTHealth Houston, established under HB 2090 in Sept. 2021) pulls from 120+ payors covering ~60% of Texans' medical coverage (80% incl. pharmacy/dental). But QRE applications only opened January 2026, with data access starting March 2026 — roughly 4.5 years of data accumulated before anyone outside could query it.

So when a vendor cites "Texas cost variation," ask: from what source, and as of when? Independent TX pricing data has been a black box until this spring — a gap any honest TX-focused direct-contracting pitch should name, not gloss over.
