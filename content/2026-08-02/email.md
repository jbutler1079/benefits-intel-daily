Hi {{first_name}},

Five data points crossed our desk this week. They look unrelated. They aren't — together they say 2026 cost growth is structural, which changes what actually works to contain it.

**1. Your reinsurance bill outruns your claims — on purpose.**
The 2025 Aegis Risk / IFEBP Stop-Loss Survey (1,268 plan sponsors) puts 2025–2026 renewals at 8.8%–10.1%, vs. ~7–8% medical trend. Stop-loss only pays above your deductible, so a small claims rise lands as a big premium rise. It compounds every year.

**2. Same building, same code, 27% different price.**
Turquoise Health's analysis of CMS price-transparency files (500 services) found self-insured employers save an average of 27% moving claims from their highest- to lowest-rate carrier *at the same hospital*. That's the case for unbundling the carrier from the network.

**3. GLP-1s are now 11.4% of plan claims** — up from 6.9% in 2023 (IFEBP, June 2026). Claims share is rising faster than coverage. Hospital repricing won't fix this one; you need a pharmacy lever too.

**4. Three surveys, one signal.** Mercer (+6.5%, or +9% if you do nothing), Aon (+9.5%, >$17K/employee), and WTW (+9.2%) — three methods, same near-double-digit story. Not a one-time spike.

**5. Texas's data black box just opened.** The TX-APCD has covered ~80% of the state since 2021, but researchers only got access in March 2026. If a vendor cites "Texas cost variation," ask what source, as of when.

**The through-line:** structural cost growth needs structural responses — unbundling the carrier, a distinct pharmacy strategy, and regional claims grounded in primary data.
